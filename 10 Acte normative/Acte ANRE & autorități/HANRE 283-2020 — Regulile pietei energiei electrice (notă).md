# HANRE nr. 283/2020 — Regulile pieței energiei electrice — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (text)]] ✅ *complete annex text* · **Electricity law in force:** [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]] · **Hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]] · **Concept:** [[Concept — Parte responsabilă de echilibrare]]

> [!warning] Currency risk: issued under repealed L107/2016
> The decision was issued under art. 7(3)(a) of [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]], which was repealed by [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]] on 19.08.2025. The text is complete and currently recorded as in force, but any reliance should check whether ANRE has re-adopted or further amended the market rules under L164/2025's enabling articles.

> [!success] Annex now complete
> Earlier vault notes described HANRE 283/2020 as decision-only. That is no longer true. The full annex — the market rules themselves — is now in [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (text)]]. The consolidation includes HANRE 383/2026, in force 01.07.2026, which updates terminology and rewrites significant market provisions.

## 1. What it is

This is the operational rulebook for Moldova's wholesale electricity market. If [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|L164/2025]] creates the modern electricity-market architecture, HANRE 283/2020 is where trading actually happens.

The rules govern:

- bilateral contracts;
- the organised bilateral contracts market;
- direct bilateral contract registration;
- day-ahead market (PZU);
- intraday market (PI);
- physical notifications;
- balancing market;
- system services market;
- balance responsibility;
- priority dispatch;
- settlement;
- reporting and publication.

The decision text also contains transitional implementation duties for Î.S. **Moldelectrica** as TSO/OST.

The numbering skips titles IX–X in the visible consolidation. Treat that as a consolidation/amendment artefact, not as a substantive gap.

## 3. The market actors

Title I identifies the institutional map:

- **OPEE** — operator of the electricity market, responsible for PZU, PI, POCB and registration of negotiated bilateral contracts;
- **OST** — transmission system operator, responsible for balancing market and system services;
- **OSD** — distribution system operators, market participants only to cover losses/technological consumption;
- **FCEE** — central electricity supplier, buying from eligible renewables and urban cogeneration and reselling at regulated prices;
- producers, suppliers, traders and other licensed market participants;
- balance responsible parties / balancing groups.

Read these with L164/2025's newer vocabulary: OPEED, aggregation, active consumers, storage and trading. Some of those concepts post-date the 2020 rules and create the main currency risk.

## 4. Market segments

Title I, point 1 as amended by HANRE 383/2026, lists the markets covered:

- organised bilateral contracts market;
- directly negotiated bilateral contracts;
- day-ahead market;
- intraday market;
- balancing market;
- system services market.

This is Moldova's layered-market design. A participant can lock in energy under bilateral contracts, adjust near real time on organised short-term markets and settle deviations through balancing.

The economic logic: schedules must match physical injections/withdrawals. When they do not, balancing costs allocate the mismatch.

## 5. Bilateral contracts

Title II governs bilateral contracting.

Important points:

- participants may contract bilaterally, subject to registration/notification rules;
- FCEE contracts sit in a special regulated lane;
- OPEE records negotiated bilateral contracts;
- import/export and transit contracts require coordination with the OST;
- OPEE calculates net contractual positions for use in scheduling and balancing.

This title is where ordinary contract law meets energy-market public law. The contract may be private-law in form, but its market validity depends on notification, registration and scheduling machinery.

Cross-link: [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]] for contract doctrine; [[Concept — Parte responsabilă de echilibrare]] for the balancing consequences.

## 6. Day-ahead market (PZU)

Title III is the day-ahead market rulebook. It covers:

- participant admission;
- offer types;
- offer transmission, modification and validation;
- price/capacity pairs;
- market clearing;
- confirmation of transactions;
- contestation;
- financial guarantees and settlement;
- emergency procedures.

The consolidation expressly references the **Euphemia** algorithm as the market-coupling-compatible clearing logic. That is the clearest sign that these rules are designed to move toward European day-ahead coupling.

This connects directly to [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]]: L164/2025 transposes CACM and defines the concepts needed for market coupling, bidding zones and OPEED designation.

## 7. Intraday market (PI)

Title IV creates the intraday market. It is the adjustment market between day-ahead closure and physical delivery.

Its practical function:

- let participants correct forecasts as generation/consumption changes;
- reduce balancing exposure;
- improve renewable integration;
- support closer-to-real-time trading.

The 2026 amendment changed terminology from *piața pe parcursul zilei* to **piața intrazilnică**, aligning local language with EU-market terminology.

## 8. Physical notifications

Title V governs physical notifications. This is the bridge between commercial trades and dispatchable physical reality.

Every contract and market transaction must eventually become a schedule. The notification process tells the TSO what each participant expects to inject or withdraw. The TSO then uses these positions for system operation and balancing.

This is why trading advice cannot stop at contract execution. A valid trade still needs market registration, notification, scheduling and balancing coverage.

## 9. Balancing and balance responsibility

Title VI creates the balancing market. Title VIII regulates responsibility for balancing.

The key concept is [[Concept — Parte responsabilă de echilibrare]]. A participant or group must be financially responsible for deviations between notified schedules and metered outcomes.

The rules also require wholesale market participants to sign balancing contracts with the OST. The approving decision originally required all wholesale participants to conclude balancing contracts before the rules entered fully into force.

Balancing is the enforcement mechanism of electricity-market discipline. Without it, there is no cost for being wrong.

## 10. System services and priority dispatch

Title VII governs system services. Title XI governs priority dispatch.

Priority dispatch is now a sensitive topic because EU fourth-package design narrows privileged dispatch and pushes markets toward technology-neutral balancing. For Moldova, priority dispatch also interacts with:

- eligible renewable producers → [[Concept — Producător eligibil]];
- urban cogeneration and heat-demand constraints → [[Legea 92-2014 — energia termica si cogenerarea (notă)]];
- FCEE procurement/resale mechanics;
- L164/2025's newer market-design rules.

## 11. Settlement, reporting and transparency

Title XII covers settlement. Title XIII covers reporting and publication.

These are not administrative add-ons. Settlement is where commercial exposure crystallises; reporting and publication are where market integrity begins.

Under L164/2025, market integrity is now also tied to REMIT concepts: inside information, wholesale energy products, market manipulation and publication obligations. The 2020 rules may contain transparency mechanisms, but the REMIT legal frame is now statutory and should be checked separately in [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]].

## 12. The 2026 amendment

HANRE 383/2026, in force 01.07.2026, is visible throughout the text.

At minimum it:

- updates terminology: PPZ → PI, *participant la piața energiei electrice* → *participant la piață*;
- changes balancing terminology: *partea responsabilă de echilibrare* → *partea responsabilă pentru echilibrare*;
- rewrites the scope provision at point 1;
- updates OPEE market responsibilities;
- changes many operative provisions in the market titles.

Do not rely on pre-2026 commentary for the intraday, balancing or PZU rules without checking the current consolidated text.

## 13. Relationship to L164/2025

This is the central legal-currency question.

The rules were adopted under [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]]. L164/2025 repealed L107 and introduced:

- storage as a regulated activity;
- aggregation;
- trading;
- active consumers and citizen energy communities;
- OPEED and market coupling vocabulary;
- REMIT obligations;
- flexible connection concepts.

HANRE 283/2020 remains operationally important, but it is now a pre-L164 act in a post-L164 market. Expect re-adoption, deep amendment or replacement.

## 14. How to use this note

Use HANRE 283/2020 for:

- market admission and operational obligations;
- contract registration and notification rules;
- day-ahead/intraday offer and clearing processes;
- balancing responsibility and settlement exposure;
- market reporting/publication duties.

Do not use it alone for:

- licensing perimeter after L164/2025 → [[Concept — Licență în energetică]];
- REMIT exposure → [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]];
- connection rights → [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (notă)]];
- network technical standards → [[HANRE 423-2019 — Codul retelelor electrice (text)]], noting the annex is still missing.

## 16. Practitioner cautions

- Check whether ANRE has issued a L164-based replacement before citing.
- Check whether the market participant's activity is now newly licensable under L164/2025.
- For contracts, verify both civil-law validity and market-rule registration/notification.
- For renewables and cogeneration, follow the FCEE and priority-dispatch rules carefully.
- For trading compliance, add the REMIT layer; the market rules are not the whole compliance universe anymore.

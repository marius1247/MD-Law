# HANRE nr. 420/2019 — Codul rețelelor de gaze naturale — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[HANRE 420-2019 — Codul retelelor de gaze naturale (text)]] ✅ *complete annex text* · **Gas law:** [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (notă)]] · **Hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]] · **Regulator:** [[ANRE]]

> [!success] Annex now complete
> Earlier status notes described HANRE 420/2019 as decision-only. The full annex is now in the vault. The current consolidation records amendments by HANRE 328/2025 and HANRE 310/2026, with HANRE 310/2026 in force 08.05.2026.

> [!note] Gas is not on the same legislative cycle as electricity
> This code is still issued under [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (text)|L108/2016]], which remains in force. The L164/2025 replacement problem is specific to electricity. Still, expect future gas reform: electricity has moved to the fourth package, while gas remains structurally tied to third-package architecture.

## 1. What it is

This is the operational code for Moldova's natural gas networks. It governs access to, operation of and interaction with gas transmission/distribution systems, including cross-border interconnection points.

The code is not a supply regulation and not a market-price rulebook. It is the **network operation and interoperability layer**:

- network operation;
- quality parameters;
- operating and interconnection agreements;
- data exchange;
- allocation of capacity at interconnection points;
- harmonised tariff-structure principles for gas transmission;
- balancing of the transmission network.

For gas, this is one of the most important ANRE acts in the vault because cross-border operation, unbundling and system balancing are the practical heart of reform.

## 2. Legal basis and EU instruments

The decision cites art. 67(4) and art. 114(10) of [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (text)|L108/2016]].

The 2026 harmonisation clause records transposition/adaptation of four EU network-code instruments:

- Regulation (EU) 2017/459 — capacity allocation mechanisms in gas transmission systems;
- Regulation (EU) 2015/703 — interoperability and data exchange;
- Regulation (EU) 2017/460 — harmonised transmission tariff structures;
- Regulation (EU) 312/2014 — gas balancing.

This is the Energy Community mechanism in practice: EU network-code law enters Moldovan law through an ANRE code issued under the gas statute.

## 3. Structure

| Title | Subject |
|---|---|

| Titlul II | Natural gas networks: entry/exit points, operation, quality, information |
| Titlul III | Interoperability and data exchange |
| Titlul IV | Capacity allocation mechanisms in transmission networks |
| Titlul V | Harmonised tariff structures for gas transmission |
| Titlul VI | Balancing of the transmission network |
| Titlul VII | Final provisions |

This structure tracks the EU network-code package almost directly.

## 4. Scope

Title I, section 1 states that the code establishes rules ensuring efficient and transparent access to gas networks.

It applies to:

- interconnection points between Moldova and EU member states / Energy Community contracting parties;
- potentially entry/exit points toward third countries, where ANRE decides;
- all system operators and system users, including cross-border transactions.

It covers:

1. operation of transmission and distribution networks;
2. interoperability;
3. data exchange with system operators, including neighbouring TSOs;
4. gas flows, including interconnections;
5. capacity allocation at interconnection points;
6. balancing.

## 5. Network operation and quality

Title II begins with the list of physical and virtual entry/exit points and then sets network-operation rules.

Important themes:

- licence-holders must operate networks in compliance with technical rules;
- operators must maintain safe, reliable operation;
- network operation must respect protection zones and technical requirements;
- quality parameters and physical/chemical characteristics of gas must be determined and communicated;
- operators may refuse gas that does not satisfy quality parameters.

Gas quality is not a marginal technical issue. It affects metering, calorific value, safety, cross-border compatibility and contractual settlement.

## 6. Operating agreements and flow management

Title II also covers operating agreements between adjacent operators and management of gas flows.

The code requires:

- agreed operational procedures between interconnected operators;
- dispatch/flow-management arrangements;
- exchange of operational information;
- rules for planned interruptions, incidents and emergencies;
- publication of information by operators.

This is the legal counterpart to physical interconnection. A pipeline link is not enough; adjacent TSOs need harmonised procedures, data and allocation rules.

## 7. Interoperability and data exchange

Title III implements the interoperability code.

It addresses:

- mandatory contents of interconnection agreements;
- flow-control rules;
- measurement and quality principles;
- matching/concordance processes for nominations and re-nominations;
- allocation of gas quantities;
- emergency communication procedures;
- dispute mechanisms and amendment procedures;
- common units and data-exchange solutions;
- system availability and security for data exchange.

This title matters heavily in cross-border gas trade. If nominations, units, measurement and allocation are not harmonised, the market cannot function even if capacity exists.

## 8. Capacity allocation

Title IV implements the capacity-allocation network code.

It covers:

- standardised communication;
- calculation and optimisation of capacity;
- information exchange between adjacent TSOs;
- allocation methodology;
- standard capacity products: annual, quarterly, monthly, daily and intraday;
- annual auctions for annual capacity;
- annual auctions for quarterly capacity;
- periodic monthly auctions;
- day-ahead capacity auctions;
- intraday capacity auctions;
- auction algorithms.

The practical point: capacity at interconnection points is not allocated by informal negotiation. It is a structured, standard-product auction process.

This matters for importers, shippers and any user seeking access to cross-border capacity.

## 9. Tariff structures

Title V implements harmonised transmission tariff structures.

Do not confuse this with a specific tariff decision. Title V governs the principles and structure by which gas transmission tariffs are designed, including transparency and allocation logic. The actual tariff level is a separate ANRE tariff decision based on the applicable methodology.

Cross-link: [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]].

## 10. Balancing

Title VI implements gas transmission balancing.

Balancing in gas differs physically from electricity, but the regulatory purpose is analogous:

- system users must keep inputs and offtakes aligned;
- the TSO manages residual imbalances;
- imbalance charges incentivise accurate nominations and behaviour;
- balancing actions must be transparent and non-discriminatory.

For a shipper/user, balancing exposure is as important as capacity booking. A capacity right without nomination and balancing discipline is not commercially safe.

## 11. Amendments: 328/2025 and 310/2026

The text records current consolidation with amendments by:

- HANRE 328/2025;
- HANRE 310/2026, in force 08.05.2026.

HANRE 310/2026 is especially visible because it inserted the detailed harmonisation clause listing the EU network-code instruments.

Practical effect: do not rely on a 2019 version of this code. Use the consolidated 2026 text.

## 12. Relationship to unbundling

The gas network code assumes a world of identifiable system operators and system users. That links directly to [[Concept — Unbundling]] and [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (notă)]].

In an unbundled market, the network operator must not use network control to favour affiliated supply/trading interests. The code implements that principle operationally through:

- transparent access;
- standard products;
- published information;
- neutral allocation mechanisms;
- objective balancing rules.

This is why gas unbundling is not only corporate restructuring. It becomes real through network-code compliance.

## 13. How to use this note

Use HANRE 420/2019 for:

- interconnection-point capacity questions;
- nominations/re-nominations and matching;
- gas-quality disputes;
- interoperability obligations between adjacent TSOs;
- balancing exposure;
- transparency/publication obligations;
- capacity-auction calendar and standard products.

Do not use it alone for:

- gas supply to final consumers → supply regulation and contracts;
- licence issuance → [[Concept — Licență în energetică]] plus ANRE licensing rules;
- tariff quantum → tariff methodology and individual ANRE tariff decisions;
- electricity analogies → use [[HANRE 423-2019 — Codul retelelor electrice (text)]] and note its annex remains missing.

## 15. Practitioner cautions

- Check the current consolidation before advising on capacity auctions; auction calendars and products are amendment-sensitive.
- Capacity rights, nomination rights and balancing exposure are distinct. Do not collapse them.
- Cross-border issues may require checking Energy Community decisions, adjacent TSO rules and interconnection agreements.
- Gas remains behind electricity in acquis-modernisation terms; watch for a future L108 replacement.
- This note does not cover gas market rules in full; read with [[HANRE 534-2019 — Regulile pietei gazelor naturale (text)]].

# HANRE nr. 626/2023 — Metodologia tarifelor pentru transportul energiei electrice — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[HANRE 626-2023 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Concept:** [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]] · **Electricity law:** [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]] · **Hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]]

> [!important] Replaces HANRE 486/2017
> HANRE 626/2023 expressly abrogates [[HANRE 486-2017 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (text)|HANRE 486/2017]]. The old methodology is now historical. Use HANRE 626/2023 for current electricity transmission tariff methodology.

> [!warning] Currency nuance
> The original 2023 decision was issued under [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]]. It was then amended by **HANRE 261/2026**, in force 23.05.2026, after [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]] entered the system. The text is currently consolidated and in force, but the legal-basis trail should be checked in any formal opinion because L107 was repealed on 19.08.2025.

## 1. What it is

This is the current methodology for calculating, approving and applying the regulated tariff for electricity transmission service.

It is not the tariff itself. It is the algorithm and regulatory framework that ANRE applies when approving the actual transmission tariff for the transmission system operator.

The distinction matters:

- methodology = normative act;
- annual/specific tariff decision = individual administrative act;
- invoice/payment = contractual/regulatory implementation.

See [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]].

## 2. What it replaced

The decision expressly abrogates the methodology approved by HANRE 486/2017.

This fixes an important vault issue: earlier energy notes treated 486/2017 as the transmission tariff methodology. That is now wrong for current law. 486/2017 remains useful for historical tariffs only.

## 3. Structure

| Section | Function |
|---|---|
| Secțiunea 1 | general provisions and tariff principles |
| Secțiunea 2 | determination of regulated transmission tariff |
| Secțiunea 3 | determination of regulated costs |
| Secțiunea 4 | regulated profitability / WACC |
| Secțiunea 5 | approval, update and application of tariffs |
| Secțiunea 6 | special provisions and efficiency/quality adjustments |

The methodology is formula-heavy. For legal work, the important question is usually not recalculating the tariff from scratch, but identifying which parameter ANRE accepted, adjusted or rejected.

## 4. Tariff principles

Section 1 states that tariff determination is based on:

- stable, reliable, secure and efficient operation of transmission networks;
- coverage of minimum necessary costs for optimal regulated activity and environmental protection;
- efficient operation and recovery of investments in development, modernisation and reconstruction;
- regulated profitability;
- maximum efficiency at minimum cost;
- justified, reasonable, verifiable, non-discriminatory and transparent tariffs based on objective criteria.

This is the regulatory bargain: the TSO gets cost recovery plus regulated return, but only for justified and efficient costs.

## 5. Basic formula

Section 2 sets the tariff for year `j` by reference to:

- regulated revenue required by the TSO;
- electricity transported through the transmission network;
- regulated costs;
- regulated profitability;
- congestion-management revenue where relevant.

HANRE 261/2026 amended point 5, visible in the consolidated text. That amendment makes the 2026 consolidation the working source, not the original 2023 PDF.

## 6. Transported quantity

The methodology calculates the planned quantity transported through the transmission network as the estimated energy entering the network minus technological consumption and losses.

Losses are calculated using a five-year average relative value multiplied by estimated input energy.

This matters because tariff per MWh is sensitive to volume assumptions. If demand/flow assumptions are wrong, deviations feed later tariff adjustments.

## 7. Regulated costs

Section 3 defines regulated costs (`CRT`) and includes:

- depreciation/amortisation of fixed assets and intangible assets;
- regulated operating costs;
- tariff deviations from prior periods.

Operating-cost categories include:

- material costs;
- personnel costs;
- maintenance and exploitation costs;
- distribution/administrative costs;
- technological consumption and losses, where provided by formula;
- costs caused by factors outside the operator's control.

The methodology uses base costs and adjustment factors across a five-year cycle. That creates predictability but also requires deviation mechanisms.

## 8. Deviations

Tariff deviations are the difference between parameters planned when setting the tariff and actual parameters recorded later.

Relevant deviations include:

- transported quantity;
- technological consumption and losses;
- adjustment indexes for base costs;
- profitability linked to investment-plan implementation;
- uncontrollable costs.

The tariff is therefore not a one-shot calculation. It is an intertemporal reconciliation system.

## 9. Investments and depreciation

The methodology distinguishes assets and investment treatment. Investments approved by [[ANRE]] are included in depreciation and regulated profitability according to the methodology.

Section 4 also cross-refers to the ANRE regulation on planning, approval and execution of investments, approved by HANRE 283/2016.

For disputes, the question is often:

- was the investment approved?
- was it put into operation?
- is it in the regulated asset base?
- is depreciation being used for new investment as required?

## 10. Regulated profitability and WACC

Section 4 determines regulated profitability using the value of new assets and a rate of return based on WACC.

The methodology references:

- CAPM for cost of equity;
- risk-free rate based on 10-year state bonds;
- industry beta, with a CEER reference value visible in the 2026 consolidation;
- market risk premium using Damodaran data;
- income-tax rate;
- a 50/50 own funds / borrowed funds structure;
- cost of borrowed capital based on National Bank of Moldova data.

This is where a tariff case becomes finance-heavy. A legal challenge may still focus on procedural legality and reason-giving, but the economic substance sits in WACC parameters.

## 11. Five-year cycles and updates

Section 5 provides for annual tariff determination by the TSO and submission to ANRE.

Base costs are approved for a five-year period. In years 2–5, the methodology adjusts categories by indexes and efficiency factors.

Tariffs may be updated during the year if objective factors outside the TSO's control generate deviations greater than 5% from regulated costs used in the approved tariff. Examples include:

- changes in transported quantity;
- price changes for electricity used to cover technological consumption and losses;
- exchange-rate fluctuation;
- legislative changes affecting costs.

ANRE can also require recalculation if objective factors reduce the justified tariff.

## 12. Application of tariff

The tariff applies to market participants for electricity actually transported through the transmission network, including exported and transited quantities, except where the TSO participates in an inter-TSO compensation mechanism.

That application rule should be checked in cross-border trading and transit matters.

## 13. Special provisions: depreciation, efficiency, quality

Section 6 is important because it prevents the methodology from becoming pure cost pass-through.

It provides:

- depreciation included in the tariff must be used for new investments, with tariff reduction if used otherwise;
- efficiency-sharing formulas when actual costs are below calculated costs;
- quality-indicator consequences.

The quality provision is the public-service counterweight to cost recovery. The TSO cannot simply recover costs while ignoring service quality.

## 14. Relationship to L164/2025

The current electricity statute, [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]], changed the sectoral framework:

- electricity transmission remains a regulated monopoly;
- market design now includes fourth-package concepts;
- congestion, cross-border capacity and market coupling become more central;
- storage and aggregation may change network-use patterns;
- tariff methodology must be read with new statutory principles.

HANRE 626/2023 predates L164, but its 2026 amendment means it has been carried into the new era rather than immediately replaced.

## 15. Relationship to connection and market rules

Transmission tariff methodology interacts with:

- [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (notă)]] — network development, reinforcement, connection capacity and applicant-funded works;
- [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (notă)]] — market participants and transported quantities;
- [[Concept — Racordare la rețea]] — network-cost allocation;
- [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]] — methodology/tariff distinction.

The same physical network appears in all three places: connection, market operation and tariff recovery.

## 16. How to use this note

Use HANRE 626/2023 for:

- identifying allowable regulated cost categories;
- reviewing ANRE tariff approval reasoning;
- checking investment and depreciation treatment;
- understanding WACC and regulated return;
- analysing mid-year tariff updates;
- replacing all current references to HANRE 486/2017.

Do not use it for:

- distribution tariffs → separate distribution methodology;
- renewable fixed tariffs → separate renewable methodology;
- the actual current tariff level → consult ANRE tariff decisions / tariffs in force;
- connection fees and developer contributions → [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (notă)]].

## 17. Practitioner cautions

- Always distinguish the methodology from the tariff decision.
- Check whether the tariff year falls before or after HANRE 261/2026.
- If challenging a tariff, build the argument around parameter choice, statutory competence, procedure, evidence and reason-giving.
- Do not cite HANRE 486/2017 as current law.
- Cross-border/transit application should be checked against any inter-TSO compensation mechanism.

# Codul de executare nr. 443/2004 — Analysis
**Raw text:** [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Proceduri]] · **Civil procedure:** [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (notă)]] · **Administrative procedure:** [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]]
> [!note] Why this matters
> This is the code for turning judgments, arbitral awards, sanctions and other enforcement documents into recovery. It governs writs, bailiffs, seizure, sale, ranking, suspension and complaints against enforcement acts.

## 1. Scope and principles
The code regulates compulsory enforcement by judicial enforcement officers (`executori judecătorești`).
For civil judgments, [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (notă)]] determines the judgment and title; this code determines enforcement mechanics.
For administrative acts or judgments, also check [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]] and the sector statute.
Early principles: legality, equality, creditor disposition, humanism, Romanian language, bailiff independence, mandatory bailiff requirements and contestability of bailiff acts.

## 2. Voluntary performance
Arts. 91–94 create a voluntary-performance layer. Where law or court has not set a term, the creditor must summon the debtor and allow at least 15 days.
The creditor may also need a declaration of non-performance and proof of summons. Premature enforcement is vulnerable.

## 3. Enforcement documents
Art. 11 lists enforceable documents:
- court writs;
- administrative-contentious judgments, rulings and orders;
- contravention and criminal monetary titles;
- writs for arbitral awards;
- recognised foreign judgments and foreign arbitral awards;
- certain regulatory, tax and disciplinary monetary decisions;
- bailiff settlement records in specified cases.
If the document is not enforceable under art. 11, the case belongs in litigation, arbitration or an administrative procedure first.

## 4. Writs and limitation
Arts. 12–20 govern issuance, content, presentation, time limits, interruption, restoration, duplicates and correction.
Key anchors: art. 14 on content, art. 15 on presentation, art. 16 on time limits, art. 17 on interruption and art. 18 on restoration.
Always check when the title became enforceable and whether the presentation term was interrupted or lost.

## 5. Bailiff powers and costs
Arts. 21–35 regulate bailiff rights, obligations, territorial competence, transfer and recusal.
Art. 22 is the power list: information requests, asset searches, seizure, sale, calculations of interest/penalties and requests to registers or authorities.
Arts. 36–41 regulate costs, fees, honorarium and release of sums. Enforcement costs are paid with priority under art. 146.

## 6. Opening enforcement
Art. 60 is central: enforcement begins on creditor request or, where allowed, court initiative, after presentation of the enforcement document and failure of voluntary performance.
The bailiff issues the opening ruling within 3 days and sends it to the parties.
The debtor may contest opening only on art. 61 refusal grounds; the contest does not automatically suspend enforcement.
Art. 61 requires refusal where the document is outside competence, time-barred, defective, presented by an unauthorised person, premature, unsupported by required proof or already executed.

## 7. Settlement and security measures
Art. 62 permits conciliation. A settlement recorded by the bailiff can become an enforcement document and ground for terminating enforcement.
Arts. 63–64/1 allow seizure of money/assets, prohibitions addressed to debtors or third parties, restrictions affecting debtor assets and court-approved travel bans.
Measures must be necessary and proportional. Contests generally do not suspend them, so proportionality is often the debtor's main argument.

## 8. Acts, communication and suspension
Arts. 65–75 cover bailiff rulings, communication, address changes, timing, reasonable execution, explanation of judgments, search for debtor/assets and enforcement records.
Bailiff rulings are enforceable from issuance and usually contestable within 10 days from communication unless a special rule applies.
Arts. 76–87 cover postponement, change of manner/order, mandatory and optional suspension, caution, termination, return of documents and reconstruction of lost files.
Suspension often links to appeal/recourse under [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (notă)]]. In patrimonial matters, caution can determine whether execution pauses.

## 9. What can be seized
Arts. 88–104 cover asset pursuit:
- any civil-circuit asset, patrimonial right or monetary claim may be pursued unless exempt;
- art. 89 protects minimum living/professional assets of natural persons;
- pledged or mortgaged assets remain pursuable by secured creditors;
- art. 90 sets sequence: free assets and money, co-owned assets, pledged/mortgaged assets, residence last.
This is where [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]] property and security concepts become operational.

## 10. Seizure and sale
Arts. 115–143 govern seizure protocol, valuation, custody, auction, repeated auction, creditor takeover, court confirmation for registrable assets and auction contests.
Anchors: art. 118 seizure/removal protocol, art. 126 sale timing, art. 136 court confirmation for registrable property, art. 142 auction-result contests.

## 11. Ranking and distribution
Arts. 144–146 set distribution: first category family/labour/personal injury and social contributions; second category public-budget and certain offence/IP/legal-assistance claims; third category all others.
Enforcement costs come first. Pledge and mortgage claims are paid preferentially from the encumbered asset proceeds.

## 12. Special enforcement and complaints
Special chapters cover eviction, acts only the debtor can perform, reinstatement at work, child domicile, delivery of money/property, foreign judgments/awards and return of enforcement after annulment.
Arts. 161–164 govern complaints: parties and affected third parties may contest bailiff acts, actions or inaction; the general term is 15 days from knowledge or deemed knowledge; the court is where the bailiff office sits; the case is judged under CPC within 30 days.
Legal acts are not annulled for purely formal defects. Third-party ownership claims against seizure use contentious procedure.

## Practitioner cautions
- Confirm art. 11 enforceability and limitation before filing.
- Preserve proof of voluntary summons and non-performance.
- Treat seizure, auction, distribution and complaint deadlines separately.
- For arbitral awards, read with [[Legea 23-2008 — arbitraj (notă)]] and CPC arts. 482–486.

# Codul de procedură civilă nr. 225/2003 — Analysis
**Raw text:** [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Proceduri]] · **Administrative counterpart:** [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]] · **Enforcement:** [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (text)]] · **Civil code:** [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]]
> [!note] Why this matters
> The CPC is the default procedure for private-law disputes. It turns rights under [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]] and sector statutes into claims, evidence, interim measures, judgments, appeals and enforcement titles.

## 1. Scope
The code governs civil, commercial, family, labour, housing, property and other ordinary-court disputes, plus special proceedings and cases with foreign elements.
It is procedural law. Use [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]] for substantive private rights and [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]] for public-authority acts unless that code refers back to CPC.
Arts. 1–6 frame access to justice; court access cannot be refused because the law is unclear. Pre-dispute waiver of court access is ineffective except for lawful arbitration under [[Legea 23-2008 — arbitraj (notă)]].

## 2. Principles and courts
Core principles: legality, adversarial process, equality of arms, publicity, language, oral examination and immediacy.
Chapter IV is the filing gateway: ordinary jurisdiction, competence of judecătorii/curți de apel, territorial forums, claimant-choice forums, exclusive jurisdiction, connected claims, transfer and conflicts of competence.
In regulated sectors, classify first: civil contract, administrative act, arbitration clause or enforcement issue.

## 3. Participants
Useful anchors:
- art. 55 — participants;
- art. 59 — parties;
- art. 60 — party rights;
- arts. 62–63 — joinder;
- arts. 65–69 — intervention;
- arts. 80–81 — representative powers.
Specific authority is needed for filing, waiver, settlement, recognition, appeal, recourse, enforcement-title receipt and submission to arbitration.

## 4. Costs, service and deadlines
Chapters VII–IX govern state duty, stamp duty, litigation expenses, citation/service and procedural terms.
Art. 116 governs restoration of missed procedural terms, but some later appeal/recourse terms are strict. Deadline analysis is often outcome-determinative.

## 5. Evidence
Chapter X is the evidence framework:
- art. 117 — evidence;
- art. 118 — burden of proof;
- art. 119 — presentation/request for evidence;
- arts. 121–122 — relevance and admissibility;
- art. 123 — facts not requiring proof;
- arts. 127–129 — securing evidence;
- art. 130 — judicial assessment.
The statement of claim and preparation stage must already contain the factual theory and available proof. Appeal is not a full evidentiary reset.

## 6. Claim and first instance
Art. 166 is the claim checklist: court, parties, contacts, violated right, facts/law, evidence, relief, value, prior procedure and annexes.
Art. 167 lists annexes: copies, identity, duty proof, supporting documents, prior-procedure proof, authority to represent and evidence/expert requests.
Arts. 168–173 cover allocation, refusal, return, leaving without movement and counterclaims.
After filing: preparation (arts. 183–191), hearings/evidence (arts. 192–235), judgment (arts. 236–258), suspension/termination/leaving without examination (arts. 260–268).

## 7. Interim measures and enforcement bridge
Arts. 174–182 allow seizure, registry notation, prohibitions and suspension of enforcement where future execution would otherwise become impossible.
Interim measures execute immediately and connect to [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (text)]]. Wrongful measures may create security and damages exposure.
Arts. 255–258 decide when judgments are final, executable or immediately executable; the practical coercive mechanics then move to [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (text)]].

## 8. Special routes
The CPC also contains low-value claims, residual administrative-litigation references, unjustified-wealth confiscation, domestic-violence protective measures, permissive-act cases, possessory actions, judicial orders and insolvency gateway rules.
Special proceedings cover facts of legal value, adoption, child return, emancipation, missing/deceased persons and protective measures. If a true dispute of right appears, the case may shift to contentious litigation.

## 9. Appeals and extraordinary review
Appeal: arts. 357–389 cover object, 30-day term, suspensive effect, new-evidence limits, appeal limits and appellate powers.
Recourse to [[Curtea Supremă de Justiție]]: arts. 429–445 focus on legality, uniform case-law, fundamental errors, arbitrariness, unlawful composition and jurisdiction.
Revision: arts. 446–453 target final judgments affected by criminal findings, newly discovered facts, non-party rights, constitutional defects or ECHR-related grounds.

## 10. Arbitration and foreign judgments
The CPC supports [[Legea 23-2008 — arbitraj (notă)]] by governing foreign judgments/foreign arbitral awards (arts. 467–476), domestic arbitral set-aside (arts. 477–481), writs for arbitral awards (arts. 482–486) and settlement confirmation (arts. 487–492).
Foreign arbitral awards follow New York Convention logic with Moldovan filing, translation, arbitrability and public-policy checks.

## Practitioner cautions
- Classify the route before filing.
- Verify jurisdiction and prior procedure.
- Build evidence early.
- Preserve interim-measure and enforcement strategy from day one.

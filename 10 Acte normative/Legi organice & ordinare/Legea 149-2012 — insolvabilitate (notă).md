# Legea nr. 149/2012 a insolvabilității — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Societăți & Guvernanță corporativă]] · **Synthesis:** [[Societăți & guvernanță — sinteza]] · **Concept:** [[Concept — Insolvabilitate]]

> [!success] Full text now in the vault
> The complete consolidated Insolvency Law — **271 counted articles** — was ingested on 2026-07-26 from legis.md doc_id `152605`. It is the creditor-protection backstop for SRLs, SAs and most other debtors, except banks and public-law entities.

> [!danger] Governance changes when insolvency starts
> Once insolvency is opened, ordinary company organs are displaced: the right to administer and dispose of the estate moves to the insolvency administrator or liquidator, management activity is suspended, individual enforcement stops, and shareholder distributions are forbidden. See [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 74]] and [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 75]].

## 1. What it is

Legea 149/2012 establishes a **collective judicial procedure** for satisfying creditors from the debtor's patrimony through restructuring or bankruptcy/liquidation — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 1]].

Its company-law importance is not only end-stage liquidation. It changes directors' duties before filing, reverses suspect transactions, ranks claims, imposes a collective stay, and can turn director misconduct into personal liability. This is the enforcement layer behind [[Concept — Capital social]], [[Concept — Administrator]] and [[Concept — Insolvabilitate]].

The law applies broadly to legal persons, individual entrepreneurs, insurance companies, collective investment undertakings with legal personality, fiduciary companies, non-commercial organisations and succession estates. It does **not** apply to banks; the state, territorial administrative units and public-law legal persons are not insolvency subjects — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 1]].

## 3. The legal tests: cash-flow insolvency and over-indebtedness

The gateway is [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 10]]:

- the **general ground** is inability to pay;
- the **special ground** is over-indebtedness for limited-liability legal persons, assessed on a going-concern basis where continuation is possible.

[[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 2]] defines:

- **incapacitate de plată** — inability to pay due monetary obligations, including tax obligations; presumed where debt to one creditor exceeds 10 average salaries and is more than 60 days overdue;
- **insolvabilitate** — inability to honour payment obligations;
- **supraîndatorare** — for limited-liability debtors, assets no longer cover existing obligations;
- **debitor în dificultate financiară** — debtor still paying, or able to pay due obligations, but in imminent insolvency.

That distinction is critical. Financial difficulty is the restructuring window; insolvency is the filing-duty zone.

## 4. Filing rights and filing duties

Proceedings start only on an introductory petition — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 9]]. The debtor, creditors and other statutory persons can file — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 12]].

The debtor may file before full insolvency if it is foreseeably unable to pay due monetary obligations — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 13]]. This is the restructuring-friendly entry point.

The debtor **must** file under [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 14]] if:

- one of the article 10 grounds exists;
- paying one or more due creditors in full may prevent full and timely payment of other creditors;
- during liquidation under other laws it becomes evident that creditors cannot be paid in full.

The petition must be filed immediately and no later than **30 days** from the relevant trigger. Failure exposes the representative, unlimited-liability members and liquidators to subsidiary liability for obligations arising after the deadline, plus contravention liability.

> [!warning] Director deadline
> For SRLs, this aligns with the administrator's duty to file immediately and no later than one month if insolvency indicators appear and members do not cover losses — [[Legea 135-2007 — SRL (text)#Articolul 72]]. For SAs, it interacts with officer liability and intentional-bankruptcy rules — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 73]].

## 5. Intentional and fictitious insolvency

[[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 15]] creates two sharp liabilities:

- if founders, executive members or persons able to give binding instructions caused insolvency intentionally, they bear solidary subsidiary liability to creditors to the extent the debtor's estate is insufficient;
- if the debtor files despite real ability to satisfy creditors in full, guilty persons are liable for creditor losses caused by the fictitious filing.

This is one of the points where limited liability becomes conditional on loyal, timely conduct.

## 6. Opening the procedure: court order and publicity

The insolvency court decides whether to open based on the statutory ground and established facts — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 34]]. The opening order is enforceable immediately, appoints the insolvency administrator, sets the claims filing timetable, and empowers the administrator to take and manage the estate.

The order must set:

- debtor and administrator identification;
- validation hearing and creditor reporting meeting details;
- exact time of opening;
- claims filing deadline, usually up to 45 days for the definitive table;
- verification and validation timetable.

Publicity is operational, not decorative. Under [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 35]], the order is published and transmitted through interoperability to the case system, [[ASP]]/State Register, real-estate, vehicle and movable-security registers, tax authority, public-property authority, [[CNPF]], banks and other relevant bodies. Once informed, those registers freeze ordinary changes: further entries concerning the debtor or its assets are made only at the insolvency administrator's request.

## 7. The estate and the collective stay

The **debtor estate** comprises all debtor assets at opening plus assets acquired or recovered during the case — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 39]]. The estate serves creditor claims — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 41]].

The core effects are:

- control and disposition transfer to administrator/liquidator — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 74]];
- debtor acts of disposition after opening are null — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 74]];
- company management organs are suspended — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 75]];
- payments go through the accumulation account managed by the administrator/liquidator — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 75]];
- shareholder separation, share buybacks, participation payouts, dividends and profit distributions are forbidden — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 75]];
- penalties and interest stop accruing, subject to secured-creditor exceptions — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 75]].

[[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 81]] is the stay provision:

- individual enforcement by unsecured creditors is barred throughout the insolvency process;
- enforcement of estate obligations not based on administrator/liquidator acts is barred for 180 days;
- secured creditors are stayed from enforcing collateral for 180 days, with exceptions where the collateral is losing value, is not essential for restructuring/sale of business, or no plan is confirmed.

This is the collective-procedure logic in [[Concept — Insolvabilitate]]: stop the race, gather claims, preserve value and distribute by rank.

## 8. Claims ranking

Ranking starts with secured and estate claims, then unsecured statutory ranks.

**Secured creditors**: under [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 50]], pledge/security creditors have priority satisfaction from the collateral, but only up to the collateral value established by valuation. Any deficiency becomes unsecured. Priority between secured creditors follows registration priority.

**Estate expenses and estate obligations**: under [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 52]], court costs, administrator remuneration/expenses, post-opening administration obligations, contracts continued in the estate's interest and post-opening financing obligations are paid as estate obligations.

**Unsecured claims**: [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 43]] ranks chirographic claims:

1. personal injury / death claims;
2. salary claims, employee work-product remuneration and social insurance contributions;
3. Ministry of Finance credits/loans/guarantees, taxes and other public-budget payments;
3¹. consumer claims from air-carrier non-performance;
4. debts to state and mobilisation reserves;
5. ordinary unsecured claims;
6. lower-rank claims: post-opening interest on unsecured claims, fines/penalties, free prestations, shareholder/member/affiliate loans, and salary claims of the special management-person category.

Claims of a later rank are paid only after full payment of the prior rank; within the same rank, shortfalls are shared proportionally — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 43]].

> [!warning] Shareholder debt is structurally subordinated
> Loans from members, shareholders or affiliates fall into lower-rank unsecured claims. In distressed-company work, shareholder funding should be documented with this insolvency consequence in mind.

## 9. Claims filing and validation

Creditors must file a written claim application with the insolvency court — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 140]]. The ordinary deadlines are:

- 30 days for the preliminary table;
- 45 days for the definitive table;
- 90 days for fiscal obligations.

A claim not filed is not included in the definitive table and is not paid under the procedure — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 140]]. This includes claims evidenced by judgments or arbitral awards.

The application must state creditor identity, amount, legal basis and any priority/security, with supporting documents — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 141]]. The administrator/liquidator verifies value and priority — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 142]]. The definitive table is approved at the validation hearing and has the effect of a final judgment for validated claims, their amount and rank — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 143]].

Late filing is harsh. A creditor who misses the deadline loses voting, distribution, enforcement and restructuring-proposal rights unless reinstated in exceptional circumstances — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 145]].

## 10. Avoidance: suspect-period recovery

The recovery toolkit is in [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 104]]:

- acts in the last 2 years intended to hinder, delay or complicate creditor recovery;
- gratuitous transfers in the last 2 years;
- transactions in the last 2 years where the debtor's performance is manifestly greater than what it received;
- preferences to creditors in the last 6 months that improve expected liquidation recovery;
- early or undue payments in the last 6 months;
- gratuitous security for previously unsecured debt in the last 6 months;
- acts and security granted after the insolvency petition.

Affiliate transactions are separately presumed harmful under [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 105]] if made in the 2 years before the petition with, among others, a 20%+ SRL/SA member/shareholder, administrator/liquidator, close relatives or connected entities.

The limitation period for avoidance is 6 months from knowledge, but no later than 12 months from opening — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 104]] and [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 105]]. If avoided, the transferee returns the asset or value to the estate; a good-faith transferee receives an unsecured claim — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 106]].

## 11. Liquidation versus restructuring

**Bankruptcy/liquidation** is the route when the business should be realised rather than saved. The court enters bankruptcy where no restructuring plan is proposed, accepted or confirmed; the debtor fails the confirmed plan; or creditors approve entry into bankruptcy — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 115]]. The court dissolves the debtor and appoints a liquidator. The liquidator sells the estate under creditor/court control, normally within 2 years — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 117]].

**Restructuring** is a plan procedure. [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 182]] defines it as measures to pay debts through operational/financial restructuring, corporate restructuring through capital-structure change, partial asset liquidation or other lawful steps. It is applied by the insolvency court based on a creditors' meeting decision — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 183]].

Restructuring creates a moratorium — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 184]]. The plan can include:

- operational changes and discontinuation of loss-making activity;
- financing by third parties or shareholders;
- sale of assets or business as a going concern;
- amendment or replacement of security with equivalent protection;
- rescheduling, interest/penalty modifications and other debt changes;
- corporate reorganisation;
- capital increase;
- debt-to-equity conversion into shares or parts — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 190]].

Plan execution normally cannot exceed 3 years, with one exceptional extension up to 2 years if justified and the debtor complied in the first 2 years — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 190]].

## 12. Voting and confirmation of the restructuring plan

Only validated creditors in the definitive table vote; unaffected creditors do not — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 201]]. Secured, budgetary, ordinary unsecured and lower-rank unsecured creditors vote as separate classes.

A class accepts if a majority of creditors holding more than half the value of that class vote for the plan — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 202]]. A dissenting class can be overcome if it is not worse off than without the plan and a majority of voting classes accepted — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 203]].

Court confirmation is required — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 204]]. The court checks acceptance, and fair and equitable treatment for dissenting impaired classes. Minimum protections include no dissenting class receiving less than in bankruptcy, no class receiving more than its claim, and lower-rank classes not receiving more than in bankruptcy ahead of an impaired dissenting senior class.

Once confirmed, the plan modifies creditor rights and is enforceable as a final judgment if later breached — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 208]].

## 13. Director and management liability

The special management-liability chapter is central for corporate governance.

[[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 247]] defines members of the debtor's management organs broadly: commercial-company administrators, executive members, board/supervisory members, liquidators, liquidation commission members and accountants, including persons holding those functions at opening and in the prior 24 months.

[[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 248]] allows the court, on request of the administrator/liquidator, creditors' committee or interested creditor, to make those persons bear part of the debtor's debts if they caused insolvency by:

- using debtor assets or credit personally;
- trading personally under cover of the debtor;
- fictitiously increasing liabilities or hiding assets;
- procuring funds at exaggerated prices;
- fictitious/illegal accounting or disappearance of accounting/constitutive documents;
- continuing activity that obviously leads to inability to pay;
- preferring one creditor in the month before cessation of payments;
- failing to file under [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 14]];
- other intentional acts causing insolvency.

Liability is solidary where multiple persons are involved, but a person can defend against solidarity by proving recorded opposition in the collegial body or later-recorded opposition if absent from the decision — [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)#Articolul 248]].

> [!warning] The two dates that decide liability
> Establish: (1) when insolvency grounds arose and (2) when the petition was filed. The gap is the late-filing exposure; the 24 months before opening are the management-liability and affiliate-transaction danger zone.

## Practical takeaways

1. **Do not wait for balance-sheet certainty.** Cash-flow inability and the 60-day/10-average-salary presumption can trigger action.
2. **Directors should minute solvency analysis.** If the company continues trading, the file needs a reasoned basis.
3. **File within 30 days once grounds exist.** Late filing creates subsidiary liability for post-deadline obligations.
4. **Stop preferential payments.** The last 6 months and the last month before cessation of payments are high-risk zones.
5. **Affiliates are presumed suspect.** Insider transactions within 2 years are vulnerable.
6. **Creditors must file claims.** Judgments and invoices do not self-admit into the table.
7. **Restructuring is timing-sensitive.** A plan after the operating business has collapsed is usually liquidation by another name.

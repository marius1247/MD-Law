# Legea nr. 160/2011 privind reglementarea prin autorizare — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Drept comercial]] · **Synthesis:** [[Drept comercial — sinteza]] · **Core concept:** [[Concept — Autorizarea activității de întreprinzător]]

> [!summary] The core rule is exhaustion
> L160 is the statute to use whenever an authority asks a business for a licence, authorisation, permit, certificate, approval, opinion or similar document. The act's central move is the **complete and exhaustive list** of permissive acts. If the act is not in the Nomenclator and the portal, it cannot be demanded from the entrepreneur.

## 1. What the statute regulates

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 1. Obiectul şi scopul legii|Art. 1]] states the object directly: the law establishes the legal framework for authorisation of entrepreneurial activity and provides the **complete and exhaustive list** of permissive acts.

The purpose is uniform procedure: authorities use a common authorisation framework for natural and legal persons seeking to carry on business or related indispensable activity. This makes L160 the procedural companion to [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (notă)]], which supplies the underlying better-regulation principles.

## 2. The key definitions

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 2. Noţiuni principale|Art. 2]] defines:

| Term | Legal function |
|---|---|
| **act permisiv** | Any document or confirming record by which the issuing authority confirms facts / conditions and the right to start, carry on or cease business activity or an indispensable related activity. |
| **autoritate emitentă** | Public structure or body empowered by law to issue permissive acts. |
| **Nomenclator al actelor permisive** | The official list, set by this law, identifying validity, fee, issuing authority and entities involved. |
| **ghișeu unic** | Mechanism allowing businesses to submit information once for all authorisation requirements. |
| **SIA GEAP** | The automated information system for managing and issuing permissive acts, accessed directly or through the government services portal. |

The definition of act permisiv is deliberately broad. It captures licences, authorisations, permits, certificates, opinions, approvals, patents and attestations. Substance prevails over label: if the document conditions business activity, it belongs in the L160 framework.

## 3. Scope and exclusions

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 3. Sfera de aplicare a prezentei legi|Art. 3]] applies to issuing authorities empowered by law to issue permissive acts for entrepreneurial activity or other legally provided activity connected with and indispensable to it.

Financial-sector licences, authorisations and certifications are outside the law's scope. Services rules also apply only insofar as not otherwise provided by the services statute. For ordinary business permits, however, L160 is the starting point.

## 4. The exhaustive-list rule

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. Dispoziţii generale privind|Art. 4]] is the central provision.

The article says authorities may request, issue and invoke against entrepreneurs only the permissive acts established in the Nomenclator in Annex 1. A new permissive act must be accompanied by review of existing acts from the same authority or covering the same risks.

The decisive rule is [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. Dispoziţii generale privind|art. 4(3)]]:

> A permissive act may be invoked or applied only after inclusion in the Nomenclator and in the government public-services portal. Economic agents cannot be held liable for practising activity without acts that correspond to the definition of act permisiv but are not included in the Nomenclator, even if those acts are imposed by the normative framework.

This is a powerful legality defence. It means an authority cannot revive an old permit requirement from a regulation, order or internal instruction if the act is not in the statutory list and portal.

## 5. Permits are confirmations, not discretionary gifts

The modern amendments in [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. Dispoziţii generale privind|art. 4(1^1)-(1^4)]] recast a permit as a proportional intervention tool tied to risks not covered by other measures.

The most important conceptual move is [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. Dispoziţii generale privind|art. 4(1^3)]]. Rights and obligations confirmed by a permit arise from the applicant meeting conditions expressly and exhaustively set by law. The authority's will does not create the right; it confirms the statutory conditions.

Practical consequences:

- refusal must be based on failure to meet express legal conditions;
- suspension or withdrawal must be based on express legal conditions;
- missing informal preferences, practice notes or unpublished portal items should not defeat the application;
- a permit is not a rationed concession unless the law expressly limits the number or requires a competition.

## 6. Categories of permissive acts

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. ## 1|Art. 4^1]] classifies permissive acts into three categories:

1. **licence** — confers the right to carry on a regulated activity;
2. **authorisation** — confirms certain activity rights or conditions;
3. **certificate** — confirms conformity of goods/services or knowledge/capacity of employees.

The categorisation matters because the procedural rights differ. Licences and authorisations are the core sphere for tacit approval; certificates are treated more cautiously.

For licences and authorisations the statute demands exhaustive lists of documents, fees and withdrawal conditions in law. For certificates, the law must provide documents, fee or fee-setting mode, and general conditions leading to withdrawal or annulment.

## 7. The principles of authorisation

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 5. Principiile de reglementare prin|Art. 5]] mirrors and operationalises L235:

- equality of applicants;
- transparency and predictability of permissive acts;
- transparency of authorisation decisions;
- legislative grounding of material and procedural conditions;
- fees only when provided by law or calculable under statute;
- declaration on own responsibility;
- tacit approval for missed deadlines;
- proportionality in control, suspension and withdrawal;
- one-stop-shop operation.

The "declaration on own responsibility" point is important. The authorisation regime is not built on an assumption that every fact must be proven by paper before the application is received. The applicant may declare compliance where the framework allows, and the authority must use inter-authority data access instead of shifting every confirmation burden to the business.

## 8. Application, receipt and the 10-working-day default

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. Eliberarea actului permisiv|Art. 6]] governs issuance and extension.

The applicant submits an application and statutory documents, or an application with a declaration on own responsibility. The authority must issue immediately and unconditionally a confirmation certificate showing the place and date of application; that certificate starts the procedural clock.

The authority must receive and register the application even if not all documents are attached or the fee is unpaid, unless the application lacks information needed to identify the applicant. This is a common practical failure point: returning an application at the counter may be unlawful because it prevents the deadline from running.

The default issuance term is **10 working days** unless the sector law or treaty provides otherwise. If no answer is given within the term, tacit acceptance arises.

## 9. Digital-by-default authorisation

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. Eliberarea actului permisiv|Art. 6(2^2)-(2^4)]] makes SIA GEAP / the public-services portal the mandatory channel for issuance, extension, reperfection, suspension and withdrawal, except where treaties provide otherwise.

Permissive acts and duplicates are issued as electronic documents sent to the applicant's electronic address or through the government electronic-notification service. Paper is exceptional: it is used when the applicant asks, the sector law requires it, or an international instrument requires it.

This matters evidentially. The relevant artefacts are now the portal record, the generated confirmation certificate and the electronic act, not only a wet-stamped document.

## 10. Suspension of the examination term

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. ## 1|Art. 6^1]] allows suspension of the issuance term if the application is incomplete. But the authority must immediately notify the applicant, identify the legal requirements not met, and state expressly what must be submitted or done.

If the authority asks for information or actions not expressly established by law, the suspension decision is null. The applicant must invoke nullity by written notification.

The suspension cannot normally exceed 30 calendar days from notification, with specific environmental exceptions. When the suspension expires, the authority must issue or refuse; otherwise tacit approval arises.

## 11. Tacit approval under L160

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. ## 2|Art. 6^2]] is the current operational rule for silence-is-consent.

After the legal term expires and no written refusal is issued, the requested permissive act is deemed granted by tacit approval. The procedure does not apply to certificates, nuclear/radiological activities, weapons/ammunition/explosives, narcotic and psychotropic substances, urbanism and construction, or cases where the original act is mandatory under an international treaty.

For licences and authorisations, tacit approval applies unless the sector law expressly excludes it.

The applicant uses the procedure through the government services portal. SIA GEAP automatically keeps the register of confirmation certificates and tacit-approval certificates. After expiry, the portal issues the act confirming tacit approval; it replaces the requested permissive act until the authority performs its duty and issues the act.

## 12. Refusal, suspension and withdrawal

Refusal is narrow. [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. Eliberarea actului permisiv|Art. 6(8)]] permits refusal only with proper justification based on law and only where the applicant does not meet express statutory conditions or fails to prove them during a lawful suspension.

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 10. Suspendarea şi reluarea valabilităţii|Art. 10]] and [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 11. Retragerea actului permisiv|art. 11]] govern suspension and withdrawal. Read them with [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 17. Principiul echitabilităţii (proporţionalităţii)|L235 art. 17]]: business suspension is a serious measure and generally needs court control unless a special statutory route applies.

The repeated theme is express statutory conditions. A permit cannot be suspended or withdrawn to punish non-compliance with obligations that are not in the legal list for that permit.

## 13. Fees

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 9. Taxa de eliberare/prelungire|Art. 9]] covers fees for issuance and extension. It should be read with [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 6. Previzibilitatea cheltuielilor|L235 art. 6]]: charges must be legally grounded and predictable.

If a portal or official requests payment not listed for the relevant act, ask for the statutory basis and whether the act is in the Nomenclator.

## 14. Accountability of issuing authorities

[[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 12. Răspunderea autorităţilor emitente|Art. 12]] establishes responsibility of issuing authorities. The subsequent art. 12-series provisions cover sector-specific or category-specific entries and should be checked for the particular permit.

The accountability layer is important because authorisation law often fails in practice at the front desk: refusal to register, informal extra documents, failure to use inter-authority information exchange, missed deadlines, and suspension letters that do not cite statutory conditions.

## 15. Practical workflow for clients

1. Identify the activity and ask: is a permit required by law?
2. Check the **Nomenclator** under [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. Dispoziţii generale privind|art. 4]] and the government services portal.
3. Identify the permit category under [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. ## 1|art. 4^1]].
4. Collect only documents expressly required by law / portal.
5. Submit through SIA GEAP where available and obtain the confirmation certificate.
6. Diary the statutory deadline; use 10 working days as default if no sector term exists.
7. If suspended, test whether the requested cure is expressly established by law.
8. If no lawful refusal is issued on time, invoke [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. ## 2|art. 6^2]] tacit approval.
9. If an inspector alleges lack of a non-listed permit, rely on [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 4. Dispoziţii generale privind|art. 4(3)]].

## 16. Relationship with competition and sector regulation

Permits can create entry barriers. If a local or sector authority uses authorisation to protect incumbents, the issue may also be a competition-law issue under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 12. Interzicerea acţiunilor sau inacţiunilor autorităţilor şi instituţiilor|L183 art. 12]] and [[Consiliul Concurenței]] jurisdiction.

In regulated sectors, L160 does not eliminate sector licensing, but it does constrain the form, transparency and proportionality of authorisation where the act is in scope. Always check the sector law first, then L160, then L235.

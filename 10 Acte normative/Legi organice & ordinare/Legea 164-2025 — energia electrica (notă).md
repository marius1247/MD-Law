# Legea nr. 164/2025 cu privire la energia electrică — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]] · **Synthesis:** [[Energetică — sinteza sectorului]] · **Regulator:** [[ANRE]]

> [!danger] This act repeals Legea 107/2016 in full
> In force **19 August 2025**. It is not an amendment — it is a **replacement statute**. [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]] is abrogated and survives in this vault only as a historical reference. Anything in the vault written against L107 needs re-checking here.
>
> **Transitional rule:** the new law does not apply retroactively to installations already admitted into operation under L107/2016, and projects designed before 19 August 2025 continue under the previously applicable rules. So L107/2016 remains *relevant* — as the law governing legacy positions — while ceasing to be *in force*.

## 1. What actually happened

Moldova skipped from the **third package to the fourth**. L107/2016 transposed Directive 2009/72/EC. L164/2025 transposes:

| EU instrument | What it brings |
|---|---|
| **Directive (EU) 2019/944** (as amended by 2024/1711) | The internal electricity market directive — active consumers, citizen energy communities, aggregation, dynamic price contracts, supplier switching |
| **Regulation (EU) 2019/943** (as amended by 2024/1747) | Market design — bidding zones, congestion, balancing, market coupling |
| **Regulation (EU) 1227/2011 — REMIT** | Wholesale market integrity: inside information, market manipulation, registration and monitoring |
| **Regulation (EU) 2015/1222 — CACM** | Capacity allocation and congestion management; the legal basis for day-ahead and intraday **market coupling** |

Each is adopted **in the version adapted and approved by Energy Community Ministerial Council decisions** (2018/10, 2021/13, 2022/03/MC-EnC) — the standard mechanism by which the acquis reaches a contracting party. Note the 2024 amending instruments are already included: this is a current transposition, not a lagging one.

## 2. The scope list is the fastest way to see what changed

Art. 1(4) lists what the law covers. Three entries did not exist as regulated activities under L107/2016:

- **(d) tradingul cu energie electrică** — trading as a distinct activity
- **(f) stocarea energiei** — **energy storage**
- **(g) agregarea** — **aggregation**

Plus **(l)** now expressly extends to *"asigurarea transparenței și integrității piețelor de energie electrică"* — the REMIT hook. And art. 1(1) states the purpose in terms of a **decarbonised and sustainable** system, which is a materially different interpretive frame from the 2016 objectives list.

**Practical consequence:** storage, aggregation and trading are now inside the licensing perimeter. Any client doing these things needs its authorisation position reviewed. → [[Concept — Licență în energetică]]

## 3. The new actors

Art. 2 introduces a cast that simply had no legal existence before:

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **agregare** | Combining the loads of several consumers, or output of several plants, for sale/purchase/bidding on any electricity market |
| **agregator independent** | A licensed aggregator **not affiliated to its customer's supplier** — the definition exists precisely to protect independent aggregation from incumbent foreclosure |
| **consumator activ** | Active consumer: consumes or stores self-generated or shared electricity, sells it, or participates in flexibility/efficiency programmes — provided this is **not its main commercial activity** |
| **comunitate de energie a cetățenilor** | Citizen energy community: voluntary, open, effectively controlled by natural persons / first-level local authorities / micro and small companies, with **environmental, economic or social benefit as its primary purpose rather than profit** |
| **consum dispecerizabil** | Demand response — load modification in response to market signals or price |
| **instalație de stocare a energiei** | Energy storage facility |
| **flexibilitate** | The system's capacity to adapt to variation in generation, consumption and network availability |
| **OPEED** | The designated electricity market operator, for single day-ahead and intraday coupling |
| **furnizor central de energie electrică** | Central buyer designated by Government to purchase from eligible renewable plants and (for a defined period) urban cogeneration, and sell into the market at ANRE-approved regulated prices |

The citizen energy community definition is worth reading closely: the **"not primarily for profit"** condition and the **controlled-by-individuals-and-small-entities** condition are what stop it becoming a vehicle for ordinary commercial generation.

## 4. Universal service and last resort are now cleanly separated

The old law ran these together. L164/2025 defines them apart:

- **furnizor al serviciului universal** (art. 2 pt. 54) — must ensure **universal service to household consumers and micro and small companies**, as a public service obligation. A standing entitlement.
- **furnizor de ultimă opțiune** (art. 2 pt. 57) — designated to supply the final consumers **of a supplier that has ceased activity**. A contingency mechanism.

This is exactly the distinction flagged as commonly conflated in [[Concept — Furnizor de ultimă opțiune]] — the new law resolves it on the face of the statute. Note the universal service perimeter: **households plus micro and small companies**, which is consistent with the ongoing withdrawal of protection from large industrial consumers.

## 5. REMIT — genuinely new liability

The definitional apparatus is in art. 2: **informații privilegiate** (inside information), **informații cu caracter exact** (precise information), **produse energetice angro** (wholesale energy products). The inside-information definition is the EU one, extended by an express catalogue: information required to be published under the law, the network codes, the market rules and ANRE's acts; **information on the capacity and use of plants, storage facilities and transmission networks, including planned and unplanned unavailability**; and anything a reasonable market participant would use in deciding to trade.

What follows from that (*verify the operative articles at source*): registration of market participants, publication of inside information, prohibition of insider dealing and market manipulation, and investigation powers for [[ANRE]]. ANRE runs a dedicated REMIT portal.

**This is the single most important practical change for anyone trading.** Outage information is now price-sensitive information with a publication duty attached, and trading on it before publication is an offence. Generators and TSOs need an inside-information policy; there was previously nothing to comply with.

## 6. Market coupling and the regional dimension

CACM transposition plus the ENTSO-E/ACER cooperation provisions in art. 1(3)–(4) point at the real objective: **integrating the Moldovan market with the EU market through day-ahead and intraday coupling**. The definitions supporting this — capacitate interzonală, zone de ofertare, congestie, congestie structurală, comercializare în contrapartidă, element critic de rețea, alocare a capacității — are the standard EU market-coupling vocabulary, adopted essentially wholesale.

ANRE's powers at the truncation point confirm the direction: approving bilateral and multilateral **market coupling agreements** including national and regional TCMs, **designating and monitoring the OPEED**, and exchanging data with authorities in other contracting parties and EU member states.

Read alongside the post-2022 ENTSO-E synchronisation via Romania, this is the legal half of a physical integration that has already happened.

## 7. Connection — flexible connection agreements

**acord de racordare în condiții flexibile** (art. 2 pt. 2): a connection on agreed terms that include **limitation and control of injection/withdrawal**. This is the answer to the capacity-scarcity problem that the 2025 financial-guarantee reform attacked from the other side — instead of rationing firm capacity, offer non-firm capacity with curtailment built into the contract.

For a renewables developer this is a genuine change in the option set: a flexible connection may be available years earlier than a firm one, at the cost of accepting curtailment risk. Whether that trade is worth taking is now a live commercial question. → [[Concept — Racordare la rețea]]

## 8. What to do about it

1. **Re-check anything in the vault or in a file that cites L107/2016.** The article numbering has changed completely.
2. **Complete this text manually** — it is now the principal act of the domain and only art. 1 is in the vault. Art. 2 (150+ definitions) is the priority.
3. **Expect the whole tier-3 layer to be rewritten.** ANRE must re-adopt the market rules, network codes, supply and connection regulations under the new enabling articles. Every HANRE in this vault is issued under L107/2016 and is living on transitional provisions. Check each before relying on it.
4. **Clients to warn:** anyone trading wholesale (REMIT), anyone doing storage/aggregation/trading (licensing), anyone holding a pre-August-2025 connection permit (transitional rules).

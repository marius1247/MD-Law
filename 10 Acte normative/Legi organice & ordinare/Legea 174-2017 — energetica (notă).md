## 1. What it is

This is the **framework energy law**. It is not the electricity law, the gas law or the heat law; it is the statute that makes those sectoral statutes hang together. Where [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]], [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (text)|L108/2016]] and [[Legea 92-2014 — energia termica si cogenerarea (text)|L92/2014]] regulate particular markets, L174/2017 supplies the institutional architecture:

1. the common objectives of the energy sector;
2. the allocation of competence between Government, the line ministry, local authorities and [[ANRE]];
3. ANRE's status, governance, budget, transparency duties, control powers and sanctions;
4. cross-sector rules on energy accounting, statistics, security of energy objects and protection zones;
5. the post-2023 governance layer for energy/climate planning and cross-border infrastructure projects.

The best shorthand: **L174 answers who is allowed to regulate energy, and on what institutional terms.** The operational answer still lives below it, in sectoral laws and HANRE acts.

## 3. Scope and objectives

Art. 1 states the broad object: the legal framework for organising, regulating and ensuring the efficient and safe functioning of energy sectors.

Art. 2 is the real interpretive provision. It lists:

- accessibility, availability, reliability, continuity, competitiveness, quality and transparency;
- independence of the regulator;
- legal relations between energy undertakings, consumers, system users, public authorities and ANRE;
- limitation of monopoly activity, market liberalisation and competition;
- consumer protection;
- energy security;
- **energy governance and climate action**;
- cross-border infrastructure projects.

That list is the sector's general-purpose purposive clause. When a later HANRE act is ambiguous, this is one place to look for the direction of travel.

## 4. The policy/regulatory split

Arts. 4–6 separate three layers:

- **Government** — art. 4: approves strategy, programmes and state policy instruments; ensures energy security and sector development.
- **Central specialised body** — art. 5: the line ministry's technical-policy role, including drafting policy documents and monitoring implementation.
- **Local public authorities** — art. 6: local-level implementation, especially relevant in heat supply and local infrastructure.

Then comes [[ANRE]], which is not a policy ministry. It is the independent regulator. This distinction matters in litigation: a party challenging a tariff or network-code obligation should not frame ANRE as merely executing Government preference. ANRE must act inside statutory delegation and justify its regulatory choice.

## 5. ANRE independence is the core of the law

Cap. III is the constitutional document of Moldovan energy regulation.

Art. 8 establishes ANRE as an autonomous public authority. Art. 9 governs administration by a board of directors. Art. 10 governs appointment and termination of directors; art. 11 governs budget and financial activity.

The practical point is simple: an energy regulator sets tariffs, approves methodologies, licenses undertakings, imposes sanctions and resolves disputes. Those decisions are politically exposed. The law therefore protects:

- institutional separation from Government direction;
- fixed governance rules for directors;
- a dedicated budgetary basis rather than ordinary annual-budget dependence;
- transparency and reporting as the counterweight to independence.

This is not a decorative rule. It is an Energy Community acquis requirement. Any attempt to pressure tariff decisions or reshuffle ANRE leadership should be read against arts. 8–11 before reaching political conclusions.

## 6. ANRE's powers: why almost every energy file ends here

Arts. 12–14¹ list ANRE's powers and rights. The list is broad because ANRE is the tier-3 lawmaker for the sector:

- licensing and licence supervision → [[Concept — Licență în energetică]];
- approval of tariffs, methodologies and regulated prices → [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]];
- approval of network codes, market rules, connection and supply regulations;
- monitoring compliance by licence-holders and system operators;
- access to information;
- dispute handling and settlement;
- controls and sanctions.

For electricity, the specific enabling statute is now [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]]. For gas, it remains [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (text)|L108/2016]]. L174 supplies the institutional floor; the sectoral law supplies the exact subject-matter mandate.

## 7. Controls, disputes and sanctions

Arts. 15–20 matter because they turn ANRE from a rule-writer into an enforcement authority:

- art. 15 — ANRE controls;
- art. 16 — transparency of ANRE activity;
- art. 17 — legal expertise and state registration of ANRE normative acts;
- art. 18 — examination of disagreements and disputes;
- arts. 19–20 — liability and sanction procedure.

This is the sequence to use in practice:

1. identify the sectoral obligation in a law or HANRE act;
2. identify ANRE's general competence under L174;
3. check the control/sanction procedure before advising on exposure.

Do not treat ANRE letters, control reports and sanction decisions as mere correspondence. They sit inside a statutory control architecture and are reviewable under [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]].

## 8. Energy governance and climate planning

Inserted arts. 7¹–7⁴ add the modern governance layer, transposing part of Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 as adapted by the Energy Community. The move is important: energy law is no longer only market design and tariff control. It now includes integrated planning around energy, climate and infrastructure.

These provisions connect L174 to:

- national energy and climate plans;
- reporting obligations;
- monitoring of policy implementation;
- cross-sector coordination between energy security, decarbonisation and infrastructure.

Read these provisions alongside [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)]] and [[Legea 10-2016 — surse regenerabile (text)]]. They are not the same subject, but they now operate inside the same planning vocabulary.

## 9. Projects of Energy Community interest

Arts. 31¹–31⁷ are the part that was previously missing from the vault. They implement the infrastructure-project layer connected to Regulation (EU) 347/2013 and the newer TEN-E framework.

The concepts in art. 3 already point to this: *dezvoltator al proiectului*, projects of common interest in the Energy Community and projects of mutual interest with EU member states.

The practical function:

- identify projects with cross-border impact;
- coordinate national treatment with Energy Community mechanisms;
- create procedures for permitting, reporting and project handling;
- make network development a legal process, not only an engineering plan.

This chapter will matter for interconnectors, transmission upgrades and any strategic infrastructure that sits between Moldova, Romania, Ukraine and the Energy Community framework.

## 10. How to use this law in real advice

Use L174/2017 for:

- **vires analysis** — did ANRE have institutional competence?
- **independence questions** — are ANRE governance/budget/removal rules being respected?
- **control and sanction procedure** — what statutory route must ANRE follow?
- **policy/regulatory boundary** — is a decision properly Government policy or ANRE regulation?
- **cross-sector issues** — when a matter is not purely electricity, gas, heat or renewables.

Do not use it as the only authority for:

- a connection dispute → use [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (notă)]] / gas connection rules and the sectoral law;
- a tariff calculation → use the tariff methodology, e.g. [[HANRE 626-2023 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (notă)]];
- electricity market design → use [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]] and [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (notă)]];
- gas network operation → use [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (text)]] and [[HANRE 420-2019 — Codul retelelor de gaze naturale (notă)]].

## 12. Open questions / watchpoints

- L174 has been amended repeatedly, including governance-sensitive parts. Always check the current consolidation before relying on director appointment/removal rules.
- Energy/climate governance provisions may become more important as Moldova moves toward EU accession and post-2024 constitutional EU-law priority.
- For any tier-3 act issued under repealed [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]], the L174 competence layer is not enough; the electricity-specific enabling basis must now be checked against [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]].

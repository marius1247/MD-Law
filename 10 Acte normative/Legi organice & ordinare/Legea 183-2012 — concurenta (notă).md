# Legea nr. 183/2012 concurenței — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Drept comercial]] · **Synthesis:** [[Drept comercial — sinteza]] · **Authority:** [[Consiliul Concurenței]]

> [!summary] The competition statute is Moldova's market-conduct code
> This is not a consumer-protection act and not merely an unfair-trading statute. It is the Moldovan analogue of EU antitrust: anticompetitive agreements, hard-core cartels, abuse of dominance, merger control, unfair competition, public-authority restraints, institutional powers and sanctions.

> [!important] EU alignment is on the face of the act
> The preamble says the law transposes **arts. 101-106 TFEU**, Regulation (EC) 1/2003 and, partially, Regulation (EC) 139/2004. Moldovan interpretation should therefore start with the statutory text and then use EU competition doctrine as persuasive guidance where the wording mirrors the acquis.

## 1. What the law does

The scope clause is broad. [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 1. Obiectul şi scopul prezentei legi|Art. 1]] frames the act as the legal framework for protecting competition, preventing and counteracting anticompetitive practices and unfair competition, controlling concentrations, defining the activity and competence of [[Consiliul Concurenței]], and setting liability.

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 2. Sfera de aplicare a prezentei legi|Art. 2]] then makes the law effects-based: it applies to actions or omissions that have as their object or effect the restriction, prevention or distortion of competition in Moldova, and to unfair-competition acts. The key point is territorial effect, not merely place of contracting.

The basic principle in [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 3. Principiile de bază ale concurenţei|art. 3]] is constitutional in tone: freedom of enterprise, protection of fair competition and protection against anticompetitive practices and unfair competition. Public intervention is not excluded, but exceptional intervention must be notified to the Competition Council for effects analysis.

## 2. Definitions that matter in practice

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 4. Noţiuni principale|Art. 4]] is unusually important because it imports the EU competition vocabulary:

| Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **acord** | Any common will, written or oral; no formal contract is needed. |
| **acord orizontal / vertical** | The first screen for Art. 5 analysis: competitor coordination is treated more severely than supply-chain restraints. |
| **concentrare economică** | Durable change of control, not merely acquisition of shares. |
| **concurență neloială** | Separate head of liability: acts contrary to honest commercial usages. |
| **poziție dominantă** | Economic power allowing independent behaviour toward competitors, customers and consumers. → [[Concept — Poziție dominantă]] |
| **trucare a ofertelor** | Bid rigging is expressly defined as cartel conduct in tenders. |
| **notificare / autorizare** | Merger and state-aid language: the Council authorises where the law requires prior review. |

The definition of "drepturi exclusive" matters for regulated sectors and concessions. Exclusive rights granted by the state may make the holder dominant in the relevant market, and sector regulation does not displace ex post competition enforcement.

## 3. Anticompetitive agreements — the Art. 101 layer

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 5. Interzicerea acordurilor anticoncurenţiale|Art. 5]] is the central prohibition. It bans agreements, association decisions and concerted practices that have as object or effect the prevention, restriction or distortion of competition in Moldova or a part of it.

The statute lists the classic restrictions:

- price fixing;
- output, marketing, technical-development or investment limits;
- market or source sharing;
- bid rigging;
- market access restrictions or unjustified refusals to buy/sell;
- discrimination in equivalent transactions;
- tying unrelated obligations to contract conclusion.

Two practical rules matter. First, prohibited agreements are **void by law** under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 5. Interzicerea acordurilor anticoncurenţiale|art. 5(2)]]. Secondly, for object restrictions the Council does not need to prove actual anticompetitive effects: [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 5. Interzicerea acordurilor anticoncurenţiale|art. 5(5)]] adopts the EU "restriction by object" logic.

## 4. Exemptions and de minimis are real, but narrow

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 6. Exceptarea acordurilor anticoncurenţiale|Art. 6]] provides the four cumulative exemption conditions:

1. production/distribution improvement or technical/economic progress;
2. consumers receive a fair share of the benefit;
3. restrictions are indispensable;
4. competition is not eliminated for a significant part of the products concerned.

There is no routine notification-and-clearance system for agreements. If an undertaking invokes exemption, it bears the burden of proving the conditions. Block exemptions may be adopted by [[Consiliul Concurenței]], but reliance on them must still be documented.

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 8. Acordurile anticoncurenţiale de importanţă minoră|Art. 8]] creates de minimis safe harbours: 10% for horizontal agreements, 15% for vertical agreements, and 5% where parallel networks create cumulative foreclosure. But [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 9. Acordurile de importanţă minoră interzise|art. 9]] removes the safe harbour for hard-core horizontal restraints and serious vertical restraints.

## 5. Cartels — the hard-core category

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 7. Interzicerea cartelurilor dure|Art. 7]] defines the **cartel dur**: a horizontal agreement, excluding minor-importance agreements, whose object is price fixing, output/sales limitation, market/customer sharing, or bid rigging.

The statute says two things that are decisive:

- hard-core cartels are presumed by their nature to restrict competition;
- they do **not** benefit from [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 6. Exceptarea acordurilor anticoncurenţiale|art. 6]] exemptions.

In practice the highest-risk setting is public procurement. Bid rigging sits simultaneously in competition law and procurement law, and the definition in [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 4. Noţiuni principale|art. 4]] makes tender coordination a cartel even if the parties call it subcontracting, market discipline or "cover bidding."

## 6. Dominance — power is lawful, abuse is not

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 10. Poziţia dominant|Art. 10]] defines dominance as a position that may be individual or collective. It is assessed by competitive constraints:

- actual competitors and market shares;
- credible expansion or entry;
- countervailing buyer power.

There is a rebuttable presumption of dominance above **50%** market share, and undertakings vested with exclusive rights are treated as dominant on the relevant market covered by those rights. → [[Concept — Poziție dominantă]]

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 11. Interzicerea abuzului de poziţie dominant|Art. 11]] prohibits use of dominance where it may affect competition or harm collective consumer interests. Examples include unfair prices, output limitation, discriminatory conditions, tying, excessive or predatory prices, unjustified refusal to deal and unjustified termination.

The statute also contains an efficiency / objective-necessity defence. The dominant undertaking must prove that the conduct is objectively necessary or produces significant efficiencies, is indispensable and proportionate, and does not eliminate effective competition. That is a demanding evidential burden; it is not a general "business justification" escape.

## 7. Public authorities can distort competition too

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 12. Interzicerea acţiunilor sau inacţiunilor autorităţilor şi instituţiilor|Art. 12]] prohibits central and local public authorities from restricting competition by limiting commercial rights, granting discriminatory conditions or privileges not provided by law, imposing unlawful restrictions, or forcing undertakings to associate or concentrate.

This is crucial for regulated and municipal markets: the defendant may be an authority, not an undertaking. If the authority does not comply with a Council decision, [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 13. Modul de executare a deciziilor Consiliului Concurenţei|art. 13]] lets the Council go to administrative court to annul the anticompetitive act or compel action.

## 8. Unfair competition — private injury, public route

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 14. Interzicerea concurenţei neloiale|Art. 14]] prohibits unfair competition and gives the Council jurisdiction on complaint by the undertaking whose legitimate interests were harmed.

The catalogue is specific:

- [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 15. Discreditarea concurenţilor|art. 15]] — discrediting competitors;
- [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 16. Instigarea la rezilierea contractului cu concurentul|art. 16]] — inducing unjustified termination or non-performance of a competitor's contract;
- [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 17. Obţinerea şi/sau folosirea ilegală a secretului comercial|art. 17]] — obtaining or using trade secrets without consent;
- [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 18. Deturnarea clientelei concurentului|art. 18]] — diverting customers through misleading information;
- [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 19. Confuzia|art. 19]] — creating confusion with a competitor, its products or activity.

The Council complaint deadline is short: 6 months from knowledge or constructive knowledge under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 14. Interzicerea concurenţei neloiale|art. 14(5)-(6)]]. Missing it bars Council examination but does not necessarily bar court action within the general civil limitation period.

## 9. Merger control — mandatory notification and standstill

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 20. Prevederi generale privind concentrarea economică|Art. 20]] defines concentration as a durable change of control through merger, acquisition of control, or a full-function joint venture. It imposes the standstill rule: a notifiable concentration cannot be implemented before it is declared compatible or deemed authorised.

The notification thresholds are in [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 22. Notificarea concentrărilor economice|art. 22(1)]]:

- combined turnover of the undertakings concerned in the previous year exceeds **MDL 25,000,000**; and
- at least two undertakings each had Moldovan turnover above **MDL 10,000,000** in the previous year.

Rates and thresholds should always be verified in the current consolidation before filing, but these are the figures in the ingested text as of July 2026.

The substantive test in [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 23. Investigarea şi evaluarea concentrărilor economice|art. 23]] is whether the concentration would significantly impede effective competition, especially by creating or strengthening dominance. Remedies and efficiencies are possible, but the parties must prove the cumulative conditions where a transaction raises serious concerns.

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 25. Competenţa Consiliului Concurenţei în domeniul|Art. 25]] creates a two-stage timetable: 30 working days for initial review and, if an investigation opens, 90 working days for phase II, subject to extensions, commitments and suspension for information failures. If the Council does not decide within the statutory period, the transaction is tacitly authorised.

## 10. Relevant market is the engine of the analysis

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 28. Piaţa relevantă şi concurenţa|Art. 28]] makes relevant-market definition necessary for mergers, dominance and agreement analysis. The function is to identify the competitive constraints that prevent an undertaking from acting independently.

The sequence is practical:

1. define the product market;
2. define the geographic market;
3. calculate shares under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 31. Calcularea cotelor de piaţă|art. 31]];
4. test entry, expansion and buyer power;
5. then decide whether the conduct or transaction restricts competition.

This is why competition advice cannot be given from contract text alone. The same exclusivity clause may be benign in a fragmented market and unlawful in a concentrated market with entry barriers.

## 11. The Competition Council

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 32. Prevederi generale privind Consiliul Concurenţei|Art. 32]] establishes [[Consiliul Concurenței]] as an autonomous public authority responsible to Parliament. It has decision-making, regulatory, prohibition, intervention, inspection and sanctioning powers.

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 34. Colaborarea Consiliului Concurenţei cu alte autorităţi|Art. 34]] draws an important institutional boundary: sector regulators act **ex ante** within regulated sectors; the Competition Council acts **ex post** across the national economy. That makes competition law a layer over energy, telecom, finance and municipal services rather than a regime displaced by them.

[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 39. Atribuţiile Consiliului Concurenţei|Art. 39]] and [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 41. Atribuţiile Plenului Consiliului Concurenţei|art. 41]] cover the Council's core powers: investigations, infringement findings, interim measures, corrective measures, sanctions, merger decisions, state aid decisions, opinions on draft acts, market studies and court actions. State aid is expressly part of the institution's mandate. → [[Concept — Ajutor de stat]]

## 12. Procedure and defence rights

Proceedings can start by complaint or ex officio under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 49. Temeiurile pentru iniţierea procedurii de examinare|art. 49]]. Complaints require a legitimate interest.

The Council has serious investigatory powers:

- information requests under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 54. Solicitarea informaţiilor|art. 54]];
- investigations under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 55. Investigarea|art. 55]];
- inspections at undertaking premises under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 56. Efectuarea inspecţiilor. Drepturile şi obligaţiile angajaţilor|art. 56]];
- inspections in other premises, including private premises, under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 57. Efectuarea inspecţiilor în alte încăperi|art. 57]], with judicial warrant where consent is absent.

Defence rights are also express: [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 58. Exercitarea drepturilor la apărare|art. 58]] guarantees the right to comment on the investigation report, access the file and request hearings. [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 60. Accesul la dosar|Art. 60]] protects file access while excluding confidential information and internal Council documents.

## 13. Sanctions, leniency and private damages

The fine architecture is turnover-based. [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 67. Reguli generale de individualizare şi determinare a amenzilor|Art. 67]] uses gravity and duration, with total turnover in the year before sanctioning as the base.

For material infringements, [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 72. Determinarea nivelului de bază al amenzii pentru încălcarea|art. 72]] sets levels up to 1%, 1%-2%, and 2%-4% by gravity, with a cap at **5%** after individualisation under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 73. Adaptarea nivelului de bază al amenzii pentru încălcarea|art. 73(7)]]. For procedural infringements the cap is **0.5%** under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 70. Adaptarea nivelului de bază al amenzii pentru încălcarea|art. 70(6)]]. Unfair competition is fined up to **0.5%** under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 77. Răspunderea pentru concurenţa neloială|art. 77]].

Leniency is detailed in [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 84. Sfera şi condiţiile de aplicare a politicii de clemenţă|arts. 84-92]]. The first undertaking to provide evidence can receive type A or type B immunity; others can receive reductions of 30%-50%, 20%-30% or up to 20% under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 89. Reducerea cuantumului amenzii|art. 89]]. The practical advice is simple: if cartel exposure exists, speed and preservation of evidence determine value.

Private damages are preserved by [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 79. Repararea prejudiciului cauzat prin practici anticoncurenţiale|art. 79]]. A harmed person may sue for full compensation, and the limitation period runs one year from the date on which the Council decision relied on became final or was upheld.

## 14. Practical checklist

1. **Horizontal contact with competitors:** assume risk until analysed under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 5. Interzicerea acordurilor anticoncurenţiale|art. 5]] and [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 7. Interzicerea cartelurilor dure|art. 7]].
2. **Distribution agreements:** check de minimis shares and serious restrictions under [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 8. Acordurile anticoncurenţiale de importanţă minoră|arts. 8]]-[[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 9. Acordurile de importanţă minoră interzise|9]].
3. **Dominant clients:** refusals to deal, exclusivity, loyalty rebates, predatory prices and tying need [[Concept — Poziție dominantă]] analysis.
4. **M&A:** test thresholds before signing; include a merger-control condition precedent and standstill covenant if [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 22. Notificarea concentrărilor economice|art. 22]] is triggered.
5. **Public procurement:** bid coordination is a hard-core cartel, not merely a procurement irregularity.
6. **Public measures:** concessions, exclusive rights, local bans and preferential treatment should be screened against [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (text)#Articolul 12. Interzicerea acţiunilor sau inacţiunilor autorităţilor şi instituţiilor|art. 12]] and [[Concept — Ajutor de stat]].

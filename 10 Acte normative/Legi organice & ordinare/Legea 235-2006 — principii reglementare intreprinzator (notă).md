# Legea nr. 235/2006 — basic principles for regulating business activity

**Raw text:** [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Drept comercial]] · **Synthesis:** [[Drept comercial — sinteza]] · **Core concept:** [[Concept — Autorizarea activității de întreprinzător]]

> [!summary] This is Moldova's better-regulation statute
> The act is not a sector law. It is the horizontal statute that tells public authorities **how** they may regulate business: through predictable legal bases, transparent rulemaking, regulatory impact analysis, legislative grounding for primary obligations, proportionality, and silence-is-consent.

## 1. Why this law matters

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 1. Obiectul legii|Art. 1]] says the object is to establish the basic principles of regulation of entrepreneurial activity and the procedure for revising normative acts according to those principles. It excludes financial activity, but otherwise it is a general business-law constraint on regulators.

The purpose in [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 2. Scopul legii|art. 2]] is expressly economic: a legal framework favourable to business and investment for social-economic development. That purpose matters when an authority tries to read its own competence expansively.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 3. Reglementarea activităţii de întreprinzător|Art. 3]] defines regulation broadly: rights, obligations, requirements and prohibitions throughout the whole life of the business, from start-up to liquidation, plus relations between regulators/controllers and entrepreneurs.

## 2. The five governing principles

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 4. Principiile de bază de reglementare|Art. 4]] gives the operating list:

1. predictability of business regulation;
2. decisional transparency and transparency of business regulation;
3. regulatory impact analysis;
4. material and procedural regulation of starting, carrying on and liquidating a business by legislative acts;
5. fairness / proportionality in relations between the state and entrepreneurs.

These are not decorative. They are legal arguments against administrative requirements that lack a statutory basis, appear only in internal instructions, impose undocumented costs, or are disproportionate to the risk being addressed.

## 3. Predictability — the legality principle in commercial form

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 5. Previzibilitatea reglementării activităţii|Art. 5]] says business regulation is carried out through laws, Government acts and normative acts of public authorities, but the key rule is in [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 5. Previzibilitatea reglementării activităţii|art. 5(2)]]: laws must set the regulatory limits for Government and public authorities, and subordinate acts cannot be invoked if they do not comply with this law.

The most useful sentence is [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 5. Previzibilitatea reglementării activităţii|art. 5(3)]]: doubts arising in applying legislation during initiation, operation or liquidation of a business are interpreted **in favour of the entrepreneur**.

That makes L235 a business-facing companion to administrative-law legality. If the authority cannot identify the legislative rule, the business should not be forced to comply merely because an inspector says "this is our practice."

## 4. Predictability of costs and documents

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 6. Previzibilitatea cheltuielilor|Art. 6]] requires payments for permits to be set by law, with the act and fee amount identified. Public services and tariffs must be expressly listed in the Government-approved nomenclature and calculated under the approved methodology.

The practical consequence: an authority cannot monetise regulation through unlisted charges, "service fees" or mandatory paid checks unless the charge is grounded in the prescribed legal framework.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 7. Previzibilitatea documentelor|Art. 7]] says technical, sanitary, standards and similar normative-technical documents are mandatory only when established by laws. This is a defence against requirements hidden in non-public technical papers or sector habits.

## 5. Transparency — rulemaking must be visible

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 8. Transparenţa decizională|Art. 8]] obliges public authorities to inform on draft normative acts and involve the private sector, civil society and individuals in drafting and decisions.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 9. Transparenţa reglementării|Art. 9]] requires free access to draft acts and publication according to law. Acts are entered in the State Register after Monitorul Oficial publication, and internet access to the register is free.

In litigation or regulatory advocacy this supports two points:

- unpublished or inaccessible requirements should not be treated as enforceable against businesses;
- consultation defects matter more where the rule imposes new costs or restrictions on business.

## 6. Permits and silence-is-consent

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 10. Transparenţa actelor necesare iniţierii|Art. 10]] states the core boundary: starting or carrying on business is subject to authorisation only where the law provides it. The same article uses a broad concept of authorisation: licences, permits, certificates, approvals, opinions, patents and attestations.

That principle is now operationalised by [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (notă)]], but L235 supplies the older and still important logic: no authorisation obligation without law.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 11. Aprobarea tacită a iniţierii şi/sau|Art. 11]] is the silence-is-consent provision. A licence or authorisation is deemed granted or renewed if the competent authority does not answer within the legal period.

The rule is not unconditional:

- a timely negative answer is not tacit approval;
- the authority must notify documentation irregularities before expiry of the term and tell the applicant how to remedy them;
- after tacit approval, the authority may require remediation of important unmet conditions, but generally cannot simply annul unless the defect gravely affects public interest, security, order or health and cannot be remedied.

For current permit practice, read L235 together with [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (notă)]]. L160 has the modern SIA GEAP / portal mechanics; L235 supplies the principle and the pro-business default.

## 7. Regulatory impact analysis

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 13. Analiza impactului de reglementare|Art. 13]] defines regulatory impact analysis as a cost-benefit justification of the need to adopt a normative act and an analysis of its effect on entrepreneurial activity, including compliance with the rights and interests of entrepreneurs and the state.

The RIA document is an integral part of the substantiation note for the draft act. This is a strong due-process point:

- if a draft burdens business without an RIA, the process is vulnerable;
- if the RIA ignores compliance cost, delay, market entry or alternative lower-burden measures, the substance is vulnerable;
- if the authority later enforces the rule as though costs were irrelevant, the RIA can be used against it.

This should be cross-checked against the Government's methodology and the work of the State Commission for regulation of entrepreneurial activity under [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 19. Comisia de stat pentru reglementarea|art. 19]].

## 8. Primary obligations must be legislative

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 14. Reglementarea materială şi procedurală|Art. 14]] says the material and procedural rules for starting, carrying on, liquidating and controlling a business must be established by laws.

Public authorities may not adopt **primary norms** regulating the starting, operation or liquidation of a business. Persons must register or request permits only where this is expressly provided by law.

This is one of the most useful provisions in the act. It can defeat:

- a permit requirement created by order or letter rather than statute;
- an inspection demand not tied to legislative competence;
- a business-condition checklist that expands beyond the legislative text;
- a sanction for failing to do something that the law itself never required.

## 9. Proportionality in control and suspension

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 15. Noţiuni generale|Art. 15]] states the proportionality principle: regulator and controller activity in relations with entrepreneurs must be proportionate to the public interest protected and to entrepreneurs' rights. Authorities must not take actions exceeding what is necessary.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 16. Principiul echitabilităţii (proporţionalităţii)|Art. 16]] applies this to control:

- control must stay within legal competence;
- control is consultative except financial and fiscal control;
- sanctions require prior verification by the head of the control body and an opportunity to challenge;
- doubts are treated in favour of the entrepreneur;
- sanctions not established by law are inadmissible.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 17. Principiul echitabilităţii (proporţionalităţii)|Art. 17]] is the suspension rule. Suspension or limitation of business activity generally requires a court decision. In express statutory cases the authority may suspend first but must go to court within 3 working days; missing that deadline annuls the suspension.

## 10. Institutional review mechanism

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 19. Comisia de stat pentru reglementarea|Art. 19]] creates the State Commission for regulation of entrepreneurial activity, supported by the RIA Secretariat. It monitors implementation, reviews RIA reports, proposes revision or repeal of acts inconsistent with the principles, and supervises monitoring of regulatory efficiency.

[[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 20. Mecanismul de revizuire a actelor|Art. 20]] was originally a one-off review mechanism for aligning normative acts after adoption of the law. Historically, that explains the statute's "guillotine" character: it was meant to purge business regulation that did not comply with predictability, transparency, RIA, legality and proportionality.

## 11. Practical uses

Use L235 when:

1. an authority demands a document or payment not clearly grounded in law;
2. a sector regulator imposes primary obligations by subordinate act;
3. a permit or renewal application is unanswered past the statutory deadline;
4. an inspector treats a control visit as punitive rather than consultative;
5. a business is threatened with suspension without a court process;
6. a draft act imposes business costs without a meaningful RIA;
7. a local authority creates market-entry conditions or restrictions not found in legislation.

## 12. Relationship with L160/2011

L235 is the principle statute. [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (notă)]] is the procedural and list-based permit statute. For authorisation questions:

- start with [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 1. Obiectul şi scopul legii|L160 art. 1]] and its exhaustive list principle;
- use [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 10. Transparenţa actelor necesare iniţierii|L235 art. 10]] and [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 14. Reglementarea materială şi procedurală|art. 14]] to argue that no permit can be demanded without law;
- use [[Legea 235-2006 — principii reglementare intreprinzator (text)#Articolul 11. Aprobarea tacită a iniţierii şi/sau|L235 art. 11]] plus [[Legea 160-2011 — reglementarea prin autorizare (text)#Articolul 6. ## 2|L160 art. 6^2]] for silence-is-consent.

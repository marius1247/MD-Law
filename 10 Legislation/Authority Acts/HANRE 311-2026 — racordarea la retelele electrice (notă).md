# HANRE nr. 311/2026 — racordarea la rețelele electrice — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Legal basis:** [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|L164/2025]] arts. 71–73 · **Concept:** [[Concept — Racordare la rețea]] · **Hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]]

> [!success] New L164-based connection regulation
> HANRE 311/2026 is one of the first major electricity ANRE acts issued under [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]]. It expressly abrogates [[HANRE 168-2019 — racordarea la retelele electrice (text)|HANRE 168/2019]]. This is not merely a consolidation of the old connection rules; it adds the new L164 logic of flexible connection, storage, active consumers, capacity queues and competitive allocation.

## 1. What it is

This is the current regulation on:

- connection to electricity transmission and distribution networks;
- modification/relocation of existing connection installations;
- issuance, modification, extension and cancellation of connection permits (`avize de racordare`);
- contracts for transmission/distribution service;
- suspension, interruption, limitation, disconnection and reconnection;
- metering and billing of network services.

For project developers, this is one of the most important acts in the vault. Connection is where generation, storage and large consumption projects become physically possible or fail.

## 2. Legal basis and repeal

The decision cites arts. 71–73 of [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]].

It expressly abrogates HANRE 168/2019. That matters because HANRE 168/2019 was issued under repealed [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]].

Practical consequence: for new connection matters after 07.05.2026, start here, not with the old 2019 regulation. Pre-existing permits still require transitional checking, but the live regulatory logic is HANRE 311/2026.

## 3. Structure

| Chapter | Subject |
|---|---|
| Cap. I | purpose, scope, definitions and connection stages |
| Cap. II | preliminary documentation, connection application, connection permit |
| Cap. III | allocation of capacity in constrained networks |
| Cap. IV | connection of active-consumer plants and storage |
| Cap. V | design, works, energisation, certificate, ownership boundaries |
| Later sections | transmission/distribution-service contracts, rights/obligations, interruption, metering, billing and special provisions |

The structure shows the policy shift: capacity scarcity is now treated as a regulated allocation problem, not as a simple yes/no technical refusal.

## 4. Scope

The regulation applies to:

- new utilisation installations;
- new power plants;
- new energy storage installations;
- modification or relocation of existing installations;
- issuance and administration of connection permits;
- relations between system operators and users for transmission/distribution services.

The explicit inclusion of **storage** is a L164/2025 marker. Storage was not part of the old L107 connection worldview in the same way.

## 5. Definitions to pin

Section 2 defines the concepts that drive the whole regulation:

- **aviz de racordare** — written connection permit setting technical-economic connection conditions and network-use conditions after energisation;
- **capacitate de racordare disponibilă** — capacity that can be allocated without restrictions or network reinforcement;
- **capacitate de racordare condiționată** — capacity available if the applicant finances reinforcement/development works;
- **capacitate de racordare în condiții flexibile** — capacity in constrained networks without reinforcement, subject to limitation/control of injection or withdrawal;
- **contract de racordare** — contract for connection works, including reinforcement/development and connection installation works;
- **convenție de interacțiune** — operational-interface document setting approved power, reduction capacity, dispatch interaction, protections and intervention arrangements.

These definitions should be cross-linked from [[Concept — Racordare la rețea]].

## 6. Process stages

Section 3 lists the connection process:

1. preliminary documentation/information;
2. filing the connection application;
3. issuing the connection permit or reasoned refusal;
4. concluding the connection contract;
5. designing and executing works;
6. energisation and certification;
7. contracting transmission/distribution services.

This is useful as a checklist. A project is not "connected" because it has filed an application, and it is not fully operational because it has a permit. Each stage has its own legal instrument and risk.

## 7. Preliminary capacity information

Cap. II, section 1 requires operators to publish and provide information on available capacity, including areas with limited accessibility and possible flexible-connection options.

The regulation asks for a granular public view of:

- total technical capacity;
- used capacity;
- available capacity;
- capacity tied up by applications or waiting lists;
- flexible-connection options.

This is a transparency answer to queue speculation. If applicants can see scarcity, the operator's refusal becomes more reviewable and developers can price capacity risk earlier.

## 8. Application and refusal

The application section sets documents, technical information and validation steps.

Important operational points:

- the operator must register or identify non-conformities within set working-day deadlines;
- a deficient application can be rejected if defects are not cured;
- a reasoned refusal must state the network-capacity problem and inform the applicant of alternatives;
- applicants refused access may complain to [[ANRE]].

The refusal is not the end of the process. It triggers the capacity-scarcity regime in Chapter III.

## 9. Capacity-scarcity menu

Chapter III begins with a menu of alternatives after a reasoned refusal for lack of capacity:

1. flexible connection;
2. connection conditional on applicant-funded reinforcement near the connection point;
3. connection conditional on applicant-funded development outside the approved plan;
4. waiting-list registration, where eligible;
5. competitive auction for transmission-network connection capacity.

This is the single most important design choice in the regulation. Network scarcity becomes a structured allocation system.

## 10. Flexible connection

Section 2 is the new headline concept.

Flexible connection is available for utilisation installations, power plants and storage installations. It allows connection when the network lacks firm available capacity, provided the user accepts special conditions limiting or controlling injection/withdrawal to preserve network safety and stability.

The conditions may include:

- maximum injection/withdrawal limits;
- time- or regime-based limits;
- temporary disconnection ordered by the operator;
- installation of compatible control and monitoring equipment at the user's cost;
- protection/automation systems enabling disconnection or power limitation;
- additional technical conditions in the permit.

The permit is issued after the applicant signs a written flexible-connection agreement, based on the annex model.

## 11. What flexible connection really means

Flexible connection gives a **conditional capacity right**, not firm entitlement to the entire approved capacity. The user uses capacity only when the network's technical and operational state permits.

The regulation is explicit: the flexibly connected user cannot claim compensation for losses caused by agreed flexible conditions.

For a renewables or storage developer, this is a commercial trade:

- faster/cheaper access to a constrained network;
- but curtailment, limitation and no-compensation risk.

This is now central to project-bankability analysis.

## 12. Conditional reinforcement and development

Sections 3–4 let applicants finance:

- reinforcement in the immediate perimeter of the connection point;
- development of network sections not included in the approved development/investment plan.

Key points:

- the operator performs/assumes works under the connection contract;
- time limits are set for works, including 12 months for installations up to 10 kV and up to 24 months for 35 kV+ works in the immediate-perimeter case;
- assets put into operation remain with the system operator;
- development costs can include solution studies and technical design, with costs approved by ANRE.

This turns connection into an infrastructure-financing negotiation under public-law constraints.

## 13. Waiting list

Section 5 creates a common waiting-list mechanism managed through an OST information system.

Priority categories include:

- utilisation installations;
- eligible producers within supported capacity;
- renewable energy communities up to 200 kW;
- active consumers with injection up to 200 kW at an existing consumption site;
- active consumers up to 200 kW outside an existing consumption site;
- renewable energy communities between 201 kW and 1000 kW;
- users requesting cancellation of flexible limitations after being put into operation;
- other OSD-network applications rejected for lack of OSD capacity.

Within categories, applications are ordered chronologically.

This is the anti-speculation logic: capacity returns first through defined priorities and chronological order, then through auctions if it remains available.

## 14. Capacity auctions

Section 6 governs competitive allocation of transmission-network capacity for new power plants and storage.

Core rules:

- auctions are organised by the OST annually by 31 March for available/new/released capacity;
- additional auctions may be organised during the year;
- auctions use online/public platforms;
- starting price is 20% of the specific financial guarantee value calculated under ANRE 277/2025;
- if aggregate bids do not exceed available capacity, capacity is allocated free to all participants;
- if demand exceeds available capacity, capacity goes to the highest-price offers;
- detailed auction procedures are drafted by the OST and approved by ANRE under art. 71(4) L164/2025.

This is a major change. Connection capacity becomes an auctioned scarce resource once waiting-list priority is exhausted.

## 15. Active consumers, prosumers and storage

Chapter IV operationalises L164's new customer-side model:

- active consumers may connect power plants/storage for self-consumption with zero injection;
- prosumer procedures are separately addressed;
- small active-consumer and community projects receive priority-list treatment;
- storage appears throughout the permit and capacity definitions.

Cross-link: [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]] for active consumer and citizen energy community definitions.

## 16. Financial guarantees

The text requires financial guarantees of good performance for certain permit-holders, including plants/storage at 1 MW and above. It cross-refers to ANRE 277/2025 for guarantee calculation.

This is the post-2025 anti-queue-blocking tool: a speculative project must carry financial cost to hold capacity.

## 17. Relationship to the missing electricity network code annex

The regulation cross-refers to [[HANRE 423-2019 — Codul retelelor electrice (text)]], especially for connection-code technical matters. The vault still has HANRE 423 as decision-only with the annex missing.

Practical consequence: this note is strong on the connection regulation itself, but technical network-code detail still needs source verification outside the vault until HANRE 423's annex is completed.

## 18. How to use this note

Use HANRE 311/2026 for:

- connection applications and refusals;
- developer due diligence;
- storage and active-consumer connection rights;
- flexible-connection agreements;
- waiting-list/auction strategy;
- connection-contract cost allocation;
- network-service contracting and disconnection/reconnection issues.

Do not use it alone for:

- licensing → [[Concept — Licență în energetică]];
- market participation → [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (notă)]];
- tariff calculation → [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]] and tariff methodologies;
- technical network-code detail → verify HANRE 423 annex.

## 19. Practitioner cautions

- Connection capacity is no longer just "first come, first served"; priority categories, flexible rights, guarantees and auctions all matter.
- Flexible connection is valuable but bankability-sensitive because curtailment is uncompensated where agreed.
- A reasoned refusal should be reviewed against the operator's published capacity information and the Chapter III alternatives.
- Pre-HANRE 311 permits must be checked for transitional treatment and any financial-guarantee obligations.
- Storage should be analysed as a first-class connection object, not as an accessory to generation.

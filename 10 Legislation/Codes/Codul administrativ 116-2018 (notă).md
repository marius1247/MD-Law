# Codul administrativ nr. 116/2018 — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Meta-law:** [[Legea 100-2017 — actele normative (notă)]] · **Civil counterpart:** [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]]

> [!note] Why this matters for the whole vault
> The Administrative Code is the procedure and judicial-review code for public administration. It is the default route for challenging individual administrative acts: licences, tariffs, permits, sanctions, refusals, procurement-remedy decisions and many ANRE acts of individual application.

## 1. What it is

The Administrative Code governs:

- administrative activity;
- administrative procedure before public authorities;
- administrative acts, contracts, real acts and administrative operations;
- prior complaint procedure;
- administrative litigation in court;
- suspension, annulment and enforcement of administrative acts;
- appeal and recourse.

It is both a **procedure code for authorities** and a **litigation code for courts**.

## 2. Scope

Art. 1 defines administrative legislation as the main legal framework for administrative relationships and judicial control over administrative activity.

Art. 2 says the code determines:

- the status of participants in administrative relationships;
- powers of public administrative authorities and competent courts;
- rights and obligations in administrative procedure and administrative litigation.

Art. 2(2) permits special derogatory legislation only where absolutely necessary and not contrary to the code's principles.

That is important: sectoral statutes can add special rules, but they do not erase administrative-law principles.

## 3. What the code does not cover

Art. 2(3) excludes:

- private-law relationships involving public authorities;
- contravention/criminal procedures;
- acts under Constitutional Court review;
- procedure for drafting/adopting normative acts.

For drafting and normative-act hierarchy, use [[Legea 100-2017 — actele normative (notă)]]. For private contracts, use [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]].

## 4. Administrative activity

Art. 5 defines administrative activity broadly as the totality of:

- individual administrative acts;
- normative administrative acts;
- administrative contracts;
- real acts;
- administrative operations.

That vocabulary matters because the remedy depends on the type of activity.

## 5. Key definitions

Pin these early articles:

- art. 6 — administrative procedure;
- art. 7 — public authorities;
- art. 8 — exercise of public power;
- art. 9 — petition;
- art. 10 — individual administrative act;
- art. 11 — categories of administrative acts;
- art. 12 — normative administrative act;
- art. 13 — administrative contract;
- art. 16 — discretion;
- art. 17 — injured right;
- art. 19 — prior complaint;
- art. 20 — administrative action in court.

The code is vocabulary-heavy. If you misclassify the act, you choose the wrong remedy.

## 6. Principles

Arts. 21–43 set principles. The most useful in practice:

- legality — art. 21;
- ex officio investigation — art. 22;
- equal treatment — art. 23;
- good faith — art. 24;
- impartiality — art. 25;
- reasonable time — art. 27;
- efficiency — art. 28;
- proportionality — art. 29;
- legal certainty — art. 30;
- reasoning — art. 31;
- comprehensibility and transparency — art. 32;
- right to be heard / legal hearing — art. 38;
- access to justice — art. 39;
- right to defence — art. 40;
- adversarial process/equality of arms — art. 43.

These principles are not abstract decoration. They are pleading points.

## 7. Administrative procedure before the authority

The code regulates:

- participants and representation;
- competence;
- deadlines;
- public-facing responsibilities and transparency;
- initiation and finalisation of procedure;
- petitions;
- administrative file;
- access to file;
- investigation and evidence;
- hearing;
- notification;
- costs.

Important anchors:

- art. 60 — general term;
- art. 69 — initiation;
- art. 72 — filing a petition;
- art. 75 — petition content;
- art. 77 — resolving the petition;
- art. 82 — administrative file;
- art. 83 — access to file;
- art. 87 — evidence;
- art. 94 — hearing participants.

For regulated sectors, the administrative file is often decisive: what did ANRE or another authority actually consider?

## 8. Administrative act formalities

Arts. 117–131 govern formalities for issuing individual administrative acts.

Key provisions:

- art. 118 — reasoning;
- art. 119 — certainty and form;
- art. 120 — structure of the individual administrative act;
- art. 124 — information on remedies;
- art. 126 — communication;
- art. 131 — correction.

If a licence refusal, sanction or tariff decision is poorly reasoned, art. 118 is the starting point.

## 9. Effects, withdrawal, revocation and annulment

The code covers:

- validity — art. 139;
- res judicata / binding effect of acts — art. 140;
- nullity — art. 141;
- withdrawal of unlawful/favourable acts — arts. 143–144;
- revocation — arts. 145–146;
- annulment — art. 147;
- restitution — arts. 150–153.

This is crucial for licences and permits. A favourable administrative act is not always freely retractable by the authority.

## 10. Administrative contracts

Arts. 154–161 govern administrative contracts:

- admissibility;
- settlement contracts;
- exchange contracts;
- consent of third parties/authorities;
- nullity;
- adjustment and termination;
- immediate enforcement;
- supplementary application of other rules.

Administrative contracts can be confused with civil contracts. The distinction depends on public-power context and statutory framework.

## 11. Prior complaint

Arts. 162–169 govern the prior procedure.

Pin:

- art. 162 — purpose of prior procedure;
- art. 164 — filing the prior complaint;
- art. 165 — time limit;
- art. 167 — resolution;
- art. 168 — authority decision.

In many administrative disputes, missing the prior complaint destroys admissibility. Always check whether it is required or excluded by special law.

## 12. Administrative litigation

The litigation part begins around art. 189.

Key anchors:

- art. 189 — bringing an administrative action;
- art. 191 — jurisdiction;
- art. 194 — limits of judicial examination;
- art. 195 — supplementary civil-procedure application;
- art. 206 — types of administrative actions;
- art. 207 — admissibility examination;
- art. 208 — respect for prior procedure;
- art. 209 — time limit for filing;
- art. 214 — suspension of execution;
- art. 224 — judgments;
- art. 225 — limits of judicial control;
- art. 228 — effects of annulment.

Administrative litigation is not ordinary civil litigation with a public party. The court's control logic is specialised.

## 13. Types of action

Art. 206 is the map for claims. It distinguishes actions such as:

- annulment;
- obligation to issue an act;
- performance/real action;
- declaratory claims;
- other administrative-law remedies.

For energy, examples:

- annul an ANRE sanction;
- oblige reconsideration of a connection-refusal complaint;
- suspend execution of a tariff or licence decision where legally possible;
- challenge procurement-remedy decisions.

## 14. Suspension

Art. 214 is the emergency provision: suspension of execution of an administrative act.

For clients, suspension may matter more than eventual annulment. A sanction, permit cancellation or disconnection-related decision can cause immediate harm before final judgment.

> [!danger] ANRE carve-outs (LP125/2024)
> [[Legea 125-2024 — modificare parc IT si tranzitii vamale (notă)|LP125/2024]] rewrote art. **214(9)**: certain [[ANRE]] decisions **cannot be suspended** — gas licence withdrawals for unbundling/independence/certification failures and gas-transport continuity decisions; plus acts under (historical) L107 art. 81¹(1) and L108 art. 108¹(5). Remap the electricity limb to [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|L164/2025]] before litigating. Former (10) was abrogated.

The legal strategy should ask:

1. Is the act executable?
2. Is suspension available — or carved out by art. 214(9)?
3. What irreparable harm or serious doubt must be shown?
4. Does special law modify the standard?

## 15. Judicial-control limits

Art. 225 governs the limits of judicial control.

In discretionary matters, the court does not simply replace the authority's technical judgment. It reviews legality: competence, procedure, facts, reasoning, proportionality, misuse of discretion and compliance with statutory limits.

This is especially important for [[ANRE]] tariff methodology, licensing and network-capacity disputes.

## 16. Appeal and recourse

The code provides:

- appeal — arts. 231–240;
- recourse against interlocutory decisions — arts. 241–243;
- recourse against judgments — arts. 244–248.

Administrative litigation therefore has its own staged review structure.

## 17. Why energy lawyers need this code

Many energy-law events are administrative acts:

- granting/refusing a [[Concept — Licență în energetică|licence]];
- approving a regulated tariff → [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]];
- ANRE sanctions;
- connection-refusal complaints under [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (notă)]];
- approval of methodologies and codes where challenged as normative acts;
- procurement-remedy decisions under [[Legea 74-2020 — achizitii sectoriale (notă)]].

Read the sectoral law for substance, then this code for procedure and review.

## 18. Relationship to L100/2017

The Administrative Code excludes the procedure for adopting normative acts. That is the domain of [[Legea 100-2017 — actele normative (notă)]].

But once a normative act exists and an authority applies it in an individual decision, the Administrative Code returns.

## 19. Practitioner cautions

- Classify the act before choosing the remedy.
- Check prior complaint requirements early.
- Ask for and analyse the administrative file.
- Treat reasoning defects as legal defects, not drafting style.
- In discretion cases, plead legality/proportionality, not merely preference.
- Preserve suspension strategy from day one.

## 20. Legal ambiguities, vulnerabilities & risk matrix

| Provision | Identified flaw / ambiguity | Practical risk | Recommended strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Art. 162 (30-day limit)** | Conflicting appeal deadlines in secondary ANRE regulations (e.g. 15 days) | Client follows HANRE deadline and misses judicial rights | Always apply 30-day CA art. 162 as overriding procedural rule |
| **Art. 214 (execution stay)** | High evidentiary threshold for tariff decision suspension | Immediate financial harm during 12–24 month litigation | File detailed financial audit proving imminent irreparable damage |
| **Art. 225 (judicial control)** | Court cannot replace technical discretion — only review legality | Weak pleading on tariff methodology disputes | Plead competence, procedure, reasoning and proportionality — not preference |
| **Art. 208 (prior procedure)** | Special laws may modify prior-complaint requirements | Inadmissible court action | Check whether normative vs. individual act before choosing route |

## 21. Client action checklist / compliance roadmap

- [ ] Log exact receipt/publication dates of all ANRE decisions for strict 30-day cerere prealabilă calculation.
- [ ] Ensure electronic petitions to ANRE carry valid advanced qualified electronic signature.
- [ ] When challenging ANRE penalty or tariff: simultaneously file art. 214 suspension application.
- [ ] Request and analyse the full administrative file before drafting court pleadings.
- [ ] Classify the act (individual vs. normative) before choosing the remedy pathway.
- [ ] For energy disputes: cross-reference [[Energetică — contencios administrativ și precedente (notă)]] for sector-specific precedents.

## 1. What it is

This is Moldova's **meta-law on normative acts**: the law about laws, Government decisions, ministerial acts, autonomous-authority acts and local normative acts.

It answers five questions that recur across the vault:

1. **What counts as a normative act?**
2. **What rank does it have?**
3. **How is it drafted, justified, consulted and expertised?**
4. **When does it enter into force, change, consolidate or disappear?**
5. **Who can interpret, monitor and re-examine it?**

Every `— text` file in the vault depends on this law in the background. It is also the technical basis for challenging sloppy legislation: lack of competence, defective consultation, incoherent amendments, hidden repeal, defective entry into force.

## 3. The definition that controls the whole file

Art. 2 defines **act normativ** as a public, mandatory, general and impersonal legal act adopted, approved or issued by a public authority that establishes, modifies or repeals legal norms applicable to an indeterminate number of identical situations.

That definition draws the boundary between:

- normative acts — general rules, indexed in the vault as act texts;
- individual administrative acts — licences, individual tariffs, permits, sanctions, connection refusals;
- exclusively political acts — outside this law by art. 1(2).

For energy work, this distinction matters constantly:

- [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (notă)|market rules]] are normative;
- a specific tariff decision applying [[HANRE 626-2023 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (notă)|the methodology]] is usually individual;
- a licence decision is individual, although issued under a normative licensing framework.

The review route differs. Individual administrative acts live primarily in [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]]. Normative acts raise hierarchy, competence and technique questions under L100/2017.

## 4. Hierarchy: arts. 6–7 are the anchor

Art. 6 lists the categories of normative acts:

1. Constitution;
2. laws and Parliament decisions;
3. presidential decrees;
4. Government decisions and ordinances;
5. acts of central specialised public administration authorities;
6. acts of autonomous public authorities;
7. acts of autonomous territorial units with special status;
8. acts of local public administration authorities.

Art. 7 then gives the correlation rule: legal force depends on the competence and status of the issuing authority and on the category of act. A superior act can modify or repeal an inferior act of the same issuer; equal-rank conflicts are resolved by the later act unless another rule applies.

This is why the energy cascade works:

[[Legea 174-2017 — energetica (notă)|framework law]] / [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|sectoral law]]
→ Government decision
→ [[ANRE]] normative act
→ individual licence/tariff/permit decision.

If a HANRE exceeds the delegation in [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]], the problem is not merely policy disagreement. It is a hierarchy and competence problem under art. 7 and art. 3(4)(b).

## 5. General, special and derogatory norms

Art. 5 is one of the most practically useful provisions:

- general norms apply broadly;
- special norms apply exclusively to specific categories;
- derogatory norms depart from the framework rule for a determined situation.

Within equal rank:

- special beats general;
- derogatory beats general or special.

This is the local statutory basis for what common lawyers tend to call *lex specialis*. It is safer to cite art. 5 than to rely on Latin shorthand.

Examples:

- [[Legea 74-2020 — achizitii sectoriale (notă)|sectoral procurement]] is special against ordinary public procurement for utilities sectors.
- [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|L164/2025]] is special against the general energy framework for electricity.
- Transitional provisions preserving pre-L164 project treatment are derogatory in practical effect.

## 6. EU relevance and approximation discipline

Art. 2 defines an **act normativ cu relevanță UE**. Art. 3(3) requires proposed solutions to be concordant with EU legislation in the field and ensure compatibility with it.

This explains the vault's energy texts: almost every major act carries an EU/Energy Community transposition note. L100/2017 supplies the procedural vocabulary for that practice:

- marking EU-relevant projects;
- compatibility expertise;
- transposition notes;
- correlation with superior law and international obligations.

The provision is especially important after [[Constituția RM (text)#Articolul 140¹. Aderarea la tratatele constitutive şi la actele de revizuire a tratatelor constitutive ale Uniunii Europene|constitutional art. 140¹]] and in acquis-heavy fields like energy.

## 7. Legislative process: arts. 20–40

Cap. III breaks law-making into procedural stages:

- art. 20 — main stages of legislation;
- art. 21 — stages of developing a draft normative act;
- art. 22 — the e-Legislație information system;
- arts. 23–24 — initiation and policy documents;
- art. 30 — the **nota de fundamentare**;
- art. 32 — endorsement and public consultation;
- arts. 34–37 — expertise, including compatibility, anticorruption and legal expertise;
- art. 40 — the accompanying file.

The key practical document is art. 30's **nota de fundamentare**. It is where the author explains problem, objective, impact, alternatives, compatibility and implementation. For contentious reforms, the note can be as useful as the statute.

## 8. Drafting technique: arts. 41–55

Cap. IV is the drafting manual:

- constituent elements of the act;
- title;
- preamble;
- adoption clause;
- general, substantive, final and transitional provisions;
- annexes;
- article structure;
- grouping of structural elements;
- language, references and cross-references.

This matters when reading Moldovan consolidations. A clause placed in final/transitional provisions is not an afterthought; it can decide temporal application, repeal, delayed entry into force and the survival of subordinate acts.

Energy example: [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|L164/2025]] is not merely a substantive electricity law. Its final/transitional provisions control what happens to [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]] legacy positions and to pre-existing HANRE acts.

## 9. Entry into force, registration and consolidation

Art. 56 governs entry into force. Arts. 57–58 cover registration, record-keeping and the state register. Art. 61 defines the consolidated normative act.

The vault's `— text` files are research copies, not official promulgation. The legal baseline remains the official publication and consolidated text in the State Register / legis.md. That is why notes should record:

- doc_id;
- source date;
- whether the file is complete;
- relevant amendments.

For ANRE acts, state registration and Ministry of Justice registration numbers are also important; many acts are not just website PDFs but registered normative acts.

## 10. Amendment and repeal

Cap. VI is essential for currency work:

- art. 62 — amendment of a normative act;
- art. 63 — general conditions for amendment;
- art. 64 — effects of amending provisions;
- arts. 65–68 — repeal, types of repeal and moment of repeal;
- art. 70 — republication;
- art. 70¹ — additional technical/systematisation rules.

This is the legal reason the vault flags abrogations aggressively. Example: [[HANRE 626-2023 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (notă)|HANRE 626/2023]] expressly abrogated [[HANRE 486-2017 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (text)|HANRE 486/2017]]. The old methodology may remain useful historically, but it is no longer the current normative basis.

## 11. Interpretation and temporal application

Arts. 71–72 govern interpretation. Arts. 73–74 govern action in time and cessation.

For practice:

- ask whether a rule applies immediately, prospectively, retroactively or only after a delayed date;
- check whether a transitional rule preserves old law for existing situations;
- do not infer retroactivity from policy intent unless the statute says so.

This is particularly important for connection rights, licences, tariffs and procurement procedures, where investment decisions often straddle old and new law.

## 12. Monitoring and re-examination

Arts. 75–76 close the loop: implementation must be monitored and acts may be re-examined. This is where "law as static text" gives way to "law as regulatory cycle".

For energy regulation, monitoring is the reason HANRE acts constantly change. Market rules, network codes and methodologies are meant to be adjusted when practice shows a defect, the acquis moves, or market design changes.

## 14. Practitioner cautions

- The statute is procedural but not optional. Poor drafting can create litigation points.
- Always distinguish **normative act** from **individual administrative act** before choosing a challenge route.
- Equal-rank conflict analysis should cite art. 7, not only general doctrine.
- Special/derogatory analysis should cite art. 5.
- In acquis-heavy fields, check art. 3(3), compatibility expertise and EU relevance markings.

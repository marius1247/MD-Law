# Legea nr. 1134/1997 privind societățile pe acțiuni — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Societăți & Guvernanță corporativă]] · **Synthesis:** [[Societăți & guvernanță — synthesis]] · **Authorities:** [[ASP]] · [[CNPF]]

> [!success] Full text now in the vault
> The complete consolidated SA statute — **110 counted articles / 103 base article numbers plus bis chapters** — was ingested on 2026-07-26 from legis.md doc_id `154811`. This is the core statute for joint-stock companies, securities-issued equity, public-capital companies and CNPF-facing corporate governance.

> [!warning] Start every legacy SA file with the capital check
> The minimum share capital is **MDL 600,000** — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 38]]. Existing companies that failed to reach the floor, and did not reorganise or dissolve, face forced dissolution. See also [[Concept — Capital social]] and [[MOC — Societăți & Guvernanță corporativă]].

## 1. What it is

Legea 1134/1997 is the special company statute for Moldovan joint-stock companies. It does not merely regulate another form of private company. It creates a company whose ownership interests are **financial instruments entered in securities accounts**, with mandatory issuer registration and securities-market oversight. That is the reason the SA is heavier than the SRL.

The definition is in [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 2]]: a commercial company whose share capital is fully divided into shares and whose obligations are secured by company patrimony. The rights and obligations of shareholders and officers, creditor protection, governance and disclosure sit inside the same statute — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 1]].

## 3. EU alignment is visible on the face of the law

The preamble states transposition or partial transposition of:

| EU instrument | Moldovan implementation signal |
|---|---|
| **Directive (EU) 2017/1132** | Codified company-law rules on formation, capital maintenance, mergers, divisions, nullity and publication |
| **Directive 2007/36/EC** | Shareholder rights in listed companies: information, agenda, electronic participation, proxy/voting mechanics |
| **Directive (EU) 2022/2381** | Gender balance for listed-company boards and executives, effective from 2028 amendments |
| **Directive 2009/102/EC** | Single-member company elements |
| Commission recommendations 2004/913/EC and 2005/162/EC | Director remuneration and non-executive/supervisory role principles |

This is the principal company-law harmonisation act in the vault. It is more EU-facing than [[Legea 135-2007 — SRL (text)]] and should be read with [[Legea 171-2012 — piata de capital (text)]] once that act is ingested.

## 4. Shares are securities; the source of truth is not the charter alone

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 12]] makes the securities overlay explicit:

- placement, circulation and cancellation of shares, bonds and other securities follow this law plus capital-market law;
- company securities must be registered in the **Register of Securities Issuers** kept by [[CNPF]];
- securities are placed only once fully paid and recorded in securities accounts at the central depository or registrar;
- the company may place only nominative securities.

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 13]] defines a share as a book-entry financial instrument conferring rights to participate in governance, receive dividends and receive liquidation surplus. [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 15]] permits ordinary and preference shares; preference shares may carry dividend/liquidation privileges and limited voting rights.

This is the sharp line in [[Concept — Acțiune vs parte socială]]: **acțiune = regulated security**; **parte socială = closed-company participation**.

## 5. Transferability, pre-emption and control

The SA starts from securities transferability, but closed/unlisted SAs may write a statutory pre-emption mechanism into the charter under [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 12]]:

- selling shareholder sends written offer to the executive body;
- executive notifies other shareholders within 3 days;
- shareholders have one month to exercise;
- sale to third parties cannot be below the offered price;
- succession, donation and enforcement of pledge are not subject to that pre-emption rule.

Control acquisitions are separately regulated. Under [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 83]], a person acquiring more than 50% of voting shares must generally launch a mandatory takeover offer under capital-market law within 3 months. Until then, voting is capped at 25% even though the full holding counts for quorum.

> [!tip] Due diligence sequence for SA ownership
> Pull the shareholder/securities-account evidence, CNPF issuer status, charter transfer restrictions, treasury-share position, control-acquisition history and any mandatory-offer compliance. The [[ASP]] extract alone is not enough for ownership.

## 6. Capital floor and net assets

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 38]] is the core provision:

- share capital determines the minimum value of net assets securing creditor and shareholder interests;
- **minimum capital = MDL 600,000**;
- capital equals the sum of nominal/fixed values of placed shares, or the sum of contributions for shares without set nominal/fixed value;
- capital is shown in the statute, balance sheet, securities accounts/registers and company letterhead.

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 37]] adds the net-assets duty. If net assets are below share capital for 3 consecutive financial years, shareholders may require the annual meeting to consider reduction, recapitalisation, dissolution or transformation. If net assets are negative, the company must publish notice and cannot issue securities. Failure to act can ground judicial dissolution.

Capital changes are procedurally dense:

- non-cash contributions require independent valuation and detailed valuation reports — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 39]];
- capital modification is a general-meeting matter and may require separate class approval — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 40]];
- CNPF registration of additional-issue results or treasury-share cancellation is a precondition to registering capital changes — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 40]];
- reductions cannot go below MDL 600,000 and trigger creditor security/early-performance rights — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 43]].

## 7. Distribution rules: dividends are board-proposed and solvency-limited

Profit allocation is split between board and meeting. The board may decide interim profit allocation within approved norms, while the annual meeting allocates annual net profit on board proposal — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 45]].

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 46]] and [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 47]] impose the capital-maintenance and payment mechanics:

- interim dividends require interim financial statements showing sufficient distributable funds;
- dividends cannot be guaranteed;
- no dividend if the company is insolvent or payment would cause insolvency;
- no dividend if net assets are below capital or would fall below capital;
- preference dividends and bond interest must be respected before ordinary-share distributions;
- payment term cannot exceed 3 months unless the public-property rule applies;
- illegally paid dividends are returned.

The rule is economically the same as in the SRL statute but more formalised because shares can trade and distribution rights attach by record date.

## 8. The governance triangle is mandatory

The SA has a deliberately dualist system under [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 8]]:

1. **General meeting** — supreme organ.
2. **Board / consiliul societății** — supervisory organ.
3. **Executive body** — current management.

Control organs are the audit committee and/or censor commission. In small SAs with fewer than 50 shareholders, the general meeting may exercise board powers, but the architecture remains more formal than an SRL.

### General meeting

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 48]] lists exclusive meeting powers: statute amendments, corporate governance code, capital changes, pre-emption limitation/withdrawal, board/censor/audit election and liability, auditor confirmation, major transactions and conflict transactions above thresholds, convertible bonds, annual accounts, profit allocation, treasury-share disposition, reorganisation and dissolution.

Meeting procedure reflects Directive 2007/36/EC:

- annual and extraordinary meetings; physical, correspondence, electronic or mixed forms — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 49]];
- 5% shareholders can propose agenda items and candidates — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 50]];
- extraordinary meetings may be requested by censors/auditor or 25% shareholders — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 51]];
- reference date, shareholder identification, meeting materials, registration, quorum, voting ballots and minutes are detailed in [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 52]]-[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 63]].

### Board

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 64]] positions the board as the organ representing shareholder interests between meetings and supervising the company. It convenes meetings, approves market value for major transactions, decides major transactions within the 25%-50% band, approves conflict transactions up to 10% of assets, confirms the registrar, approves public-offer prospectuses, approves non-convertible bond issues, decides interim dividends and treasury-share sales.

Composition rules matter under [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 65]]:

- term up to 4 years;
- at least 3 members generally;
- at least 5 where the state holds more than 50%;
- cumulative voting is mandatory when the company has more than 100 shareholders;
- executive-body members and censors cannot be board members.

### Executive

The executive handles day-to-day management and everything not reserved to the meeting or board — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 68]]. It may be collegial or single-person; the head acts without power of attorney within authority — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 69]]. It must report quarterly to the board or general meeting.

This is the SA expression of [[Concept — Adunarea generală]], [[Concept — Consiliul societății]] and [[Concept — Administrator]].

## 9. Control, audit and information

The censor commission controls economic-financial activity and is mandatory for non-public-interest entities and companies with at least 20% state/UAT capital — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 70]]. Public-interest entities follow the audit-committee/audit regime. Mandatory audit applies to public-interest entities and companies where the state holds more than 50% — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 88]].

Disclosure is not optional. [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 90]] ties company disclosure to the capital-market law and CNPF acts, and requires annual/semi-annual reports of public-interest entities to include major and conflict transactions. [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 91]] gives shareholders access to corporate documentation, including minutes, board minutes, officer lists, interested-person lists, securities documents, major/conflict transaction files, financial statements, audit/censor reports and remuneration policy/report.

## 10. Officer duties and liability

Persons holding positions of responsibility include board members, executive body, censor/audit committee, liquidation commission and anyone exercising disposition powers — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 72]]. They must act in the company's interest, avoid unauthorised competition, cannot receive company loans/guarantees, and face restrictions on gifts/services.

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 73]] is the main liability catalogue. It covers intentional bankruptcy, concealment or distortion of financial information, market-price manipulation by false information, failure to convene meetings, unlawful dividend/bond-interest payment, unfair securities transactions, misuse of assets, breach of capital-change rules, and breach of major/conflict transaction procedure. Joint decisions create joint liability unless dissent is recorded in the minutes.

> [!warning] Minutes are liability evidence
> For board and executive bodies, a dissent that is not recorded or attached is almost worthless. This matches the practical rule in [[Concept — Consiliul societății]].

## 11. Major transactions and conflict transactions

The two transaction-control regimes are the sharpest minority/creditor protection tools in the SA law.

**Major transactions**: [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 81]] defines a major transaction as assets/rights over **25% of company assets** or issuance of voting shares/convertibles over 25% of voting shares. [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 82]] sends 25%-50% transactions to unanimous approval by all elected board members; larger transactions go to the general meeting. Decisions must be published within 7 working days, and public-interest entities disclose on the website within 3 working days.

**Conflict transactions**: [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 84]] covers transactions with an interested person or affiliate exceeding 1% of assets. Interested persons include 25%+ voting shareholders, board/executive members, and board members nominated by a shareholder where the transaction is with that shareholder/affiliate. The interested person must disclose before approval.

[[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 85]] then controls approval:

- board approval if transaction value is up to 10% of assets;
- general meeting approval otherwise;
- audit entity/censor verification before approval;
- unanimous vote of non-interested elected board members;
- if more than half the board is interested, meeting approval is required;
- interested persons leave the meeting/vote and count only for quorum, not voting result;
- breach can lead to refusal, termination/rescission and damages.

## 12. Gender-balance amendments for listed companies

The new Chapter 11¹ transposes Directive (EU) 2022/2381 for companies whose shares are admitted to trading on a regulated market. By **1 July 2028**, listed companies must target either 40% of non-executive directors or 33% of all directors from the underrepresented sex — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 73]]. CNPF and the Ministry of Labour have monitoring/reporting roles. These provisions are not central for ordinary legacy SAs but matter for listed or potentially listed companies.

## 13. Reorganisation, CNPF and creditor protection

SA reorganisations are capital-market events. [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 92]] requires Civil Code compliance, CNPF normative compliance, competition clearances where relevant, creditor notice within 15 days, one-month creditor security/early-performance rights, and **CNPF authorisation** for state registration of SA reorganisation.

Merger rules in [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 93]] include share conversion, possible cash adjustment up to 10%, executive reports, publication, valuation expert report, and class approval where rights are affected. Division rules in [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 96]] require precise allocation of assets and liabilities; unallocated liabilities in division trigger solidary liability of beneficiary companies.

Dissolution is by general meeting or court — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 98]]. After creditors, liquidation surplus goes first to announced unpaid preference dividends and preference liquidation value, then ordinary shares — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 99]]. Forced dissolution is expressly available for failure to comply with capital requirements — [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 100]].

## Practical takeaways

1. **Use SA only when the securities form is worth the cost** — public offering, regulated-sector requirement, historic privatisation, large shareholder base, or a genuine need for transferable securities.
2. **The MDL 600,000 floor is a threshold issue.** Check it before governance or transaction analysis.
3. **CNPF is not optional.** Securities registration, capital changes, issuer disclosure and reorganisations all involve [[CNPF]] or capital-market law.
4. **Related-party transactions are the live litigation risk.** Build conflict checks, valuation and approval evidence before signing.
5. **Board minutes and dissent records matter.** Liability defences are documentary.
6. **For insolvency or distress, switch frameworks.** Once insolvency opens, ordinary organs are suspended and [[Legea 149-2012 — insolvabilitate (text)]] controls.

- Exact CNPF practice on capital-floor enforcement after the 1 January 2024 transition deadline.
- Whether ordinary unlisted SAs are using charter pre-emption under [[Legea 1134-1997 — societati pe actiuni (text)#Articolul 12]] or relying on transferability.
- Current list of Moldovan companies whose shares are admitted to regulated-market trading and therefore fall under the 2028 gender-balance chapter.
- Need to ingest [[Legea 171-2012 — piata de capital (text)]] and CNPF issuer-disclosure acts to complete the SA layer.

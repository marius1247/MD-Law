# Legea nr. 139/2018 cu privire la eficiența energetică — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)]] ✅ *complete (Arts. 1–30 + Annexes 1–8; consolidated LP111/2025)* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]] · **Synthesis:** [[Energetică — synthesis]] · **Implementation body:** [[CNED]] · **Regulator interface:** [[ANRE]]

> [!abstract] Executive summary & commercial impact
> **Core purpose:** Moldova’s Energy Efficiency Directive transposition — national savings targets, obligation scheme, public-building renovation, mandatory energy audits for large enterprises, ESCO / energy-performance contracts, metering & billing, and efficient heating/cooling / cogeneration.
> **Primary business risk:** (1) large enterprises that ignore a [[CNED]] audit notification face turnover-based sanctions; (2) DSOs and petroleum importers are designated **obligated parties** and may owe quarterly contributions once Government activates the scheme; (3) electricity-side demand-response / aggregation rules in this statute still cite repealed [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)|L107/2016]] — after 19.08.2025 read them through [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]].

---

## 1. Statutory hierarchy & legal foundation

* **Organic law** in the energy policy stack under [[Legea 174-2017 — energetica (text)|L174/2017]] and the NECP governance frame.
* **EU acquis:** Directive **2012/27/EU** as amended by **(EU) 2018/2002**, adapted by Energy Community Decision **2021/14/MC-EnC** (stated in the act’s transposition note).
* **2025 reform (LP111/2025, in force 03.06.2025):** rebuilds [[CNED]], creates the Energy Efficiency Fund (arts. 23¹–23³), redesignates obligated parties, and bridges EE finance to RES / Energy Vulnerability Fund (Law 241/2022).
* **Procedural context:** sanctions under arts. 28–29 → National Inspectorate for Technical Supervision; contest under [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]]. Market-obstruction claims → [[Consiliul Concurenței]] / [[Legea 183-2012 — concurenta (notă)]].
* **Buildings bridge:** renovation and performance requirements point into **Law No. 282/2023** (not yet ingested) — cite that statute at source for building-level duties.

## 3. Operative regimes by problem

### A. National targets, EE-first, NECP

→ [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 1]] · [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 5]] · [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 6]] · [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 7¹]]

National EE objectives are **absolute ceilings** on primary and final energy consumption for a given horizon (art. 5(2)). Measures — including any obligation scheme — live in the **NECP** (art. 5(4), art. 6). Art. 1 states the **energy-efficiency-first** principle, but the statute does **not** create a self-standing procedural test that every infrastructure decision must prove an EE alternative first — treat it as an interpretive principle until secondary acts harden it. → [[Concept — Eficiența energetică înainte de toate]]

### B. Obligation scheme — who pays

→ [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 8]] · [[Concept — Parte obligată (eficiență energetică)]]

**Designated obligated parties (art. 8(1), LP111 redraft):** electricity DSOs, gas DSOs, and **petroleum-products importers**. Heat undertakings dropped out of the designation.

If Government activates the scheme (art. 7¹(5) / art. 8(15)), obligated parties **contribute financially** toward annual new final-consumer savings, referenced to average annual sales/volumes (art. 8(2)). Contributions transfer to Treasury accounts — quarterly by the **45th day** after quarter-end; first tranche by **15 May** (art. 8(6)). [[ANRE]] supplies the list and volumes and publishes the obligated-party list quarterly (art. 8(7²)–(9¹)). [[CNED]] monitors payments and measures/verifies savings.

**Sanctions (art. 28):** late transfer → **50%** of unpaid contribution; repeated refusal → **100%**. Sanction payment does **not** extinguish the duty (art. 28(8)); sanctions are **not** tariff-recoverable (art. 28(9)).

> [!warning] Hard edge — tariff vs designation
> Art. 8(9) still speaks of folding contributions into electricity/gas **distribution tariffs**, heat supply tariff and petroleum price, while the LP111 definition of obligated party no longer includes heat undertakings. Check the live contribution methodology before advising a heat supplier.

### C. Large-enterprise energy audits

→ [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 19]] · [[Concept — Audit energetic]]

Large enterprises (not SMEs under Law 179/2016) must perform energy audits under the Government regulation; [[CNED]] **notifies** them (art. 19(1)). Exemption: an energy / environmental management system that already includes an audit meeting Annex 5 criteria (art. 19(2)).

**Timing (art. 30(14)):** first audit within **1 year of CNED notification**; then at least every **4 years**. Budget/CNED-financed EE projects also require an audit, except building projects ≤ **250 m²** useful area (art. 19(3)).

**Sanctions:** failure → **0.1% annual turnover**; repeated refusal → **0.5%** (art. 28(3)–(4)), applied by the National Inspectorate for Technical Supervision.

> The practical clock starts on **notification**, not on becoming “large”. Silence from CNED delays the duty — it does not erase it once notified.

### D. ESCOs and energy performance contracts

→ [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 22]] · [[Legea 139-2018 — eficienta energetica (text)#Articolul 23]] · [[Concept — Contract de performanță energetică]]

Open, non-discriminatory access to the energy-services market (art. 22). TSOs/DSOs/suppliers must not obstruct EE services or abuse dominance (art. 22(3)) — Competition Council enforcement (art. 28(7)). Art. 23(5) lists mandatory EPC contents (measures, guaranteed savings, M&V, baseline, payment, penalties, termination, disputes). Public bodies must budget next-year EPC payment obligations (art. 23(6)). Regulated energy undertakings may use EPCs; [[ANRE]] reviews investment plans and tariff treatment is capped to **confirmed monetary savings** (art. 23(7)–(12); CNED confirms savings).

### E. Public buildings & green procurement

→ Arts. 14–15, Annexes 3–4

Central specialised authorities: **3%** annual renovation rate of heated/cooled state public-domain buildings > **250 m²** that miss minimum performance (art. 14(9)), aligned with Law 282/2023. Contracting authorities must buy high EE performance goods/services/works subject to cost-effectiveness and competition (art. 15). Local municipalities need local integrated energy/climate plans — also a **finance-eligibility** condition (art. 13(3¹)).

### F. Metering, sub-metering, billing

→ Arts. 16–17³, Annex 8

Electricity/gas metering under sectoral laws + metrology rules; smart meters where technically/economically justified (art. 16). Heat: building-level meters; individual meters or cost allocators in multi-apartment buildings where feasible (art. 16¹–16²). Remote readability for new devices (art. 16³). From **1 Jan 2025**, where remote-readable devices exist: consumption information at least **monthly** (Annex 8). Free access to metering/billing information (arts. 17²–17³).

> Art. 16 still cites **L107/2016**. For electricity metering/smart-meter investment plans after 19.08.2025, route through [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)|L164/2025]] and current ANRE acts.

### G. Heating/cooling, cogeneration, demand response

→ Arts. 24–27 · [[Legea 92-2014 — energia termica si cogenerarea (notă)]]

Ministry runs the national high-efficiency cogeneration / efficient district heating assessment (update every **5 years**). Project-level CBA above **20 MW** thermal input thresholds (art. 24(5); exceptions art. 24(7)). High-efficiency cogeneration gets non-discriminatory network access and, subject to security, priority dispatch alongside renewables (art. 27) — again, reconcile with L164 redispatch / market rules.

Demand-response / aggregator hooks in arts. 26–27 are **policy-level**. Operational licensing, independent aggregators, and dynamic contracts now sit in L164/2025. → [[Concept — Parte responsabilă de echilibrare]] where imbalance allocation matters.

### H. Finance stack (LP111)

→ Arts. 23¹–23³ · [[CNED]]

**Fondul pentru eficiență energetică (FEE)** funds EE and/or RES measures via grants, guarantees, compensations. Sources include obligation-scheme money and external assistance. Art. 23² bridges to Law **241/2022** (Energy Vulnerability Reduction Fund). Avoid **double counting** of the same kWh as both EE savings and RES support (art. 8(7)(d), Annex 2).

## 4. Legal ambiguities, vulnerabilities & risk matrix

| Provision | Identified flaw / ambiguity | Practical risk | Recommended strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arts. 16, 17, 26, 27** | Still cite repealed L107/2016 | Wrong enabling chain for metering / DR / dispatch advice | Cite L164/2025 + current HANRE; treat L139 as EE policy overlay |
| **Art. 1 EE-first** | Principle without procedural test | Clients over-read a soft principle as a hard veto on infrastructure | Require a secondary act or decision that operationalises the test |
| **Art. 8(1) vs 8(9)** | Obligated-party list vs tariff-recovery language after LP111 | Heat / petroleum cost-pass-through disputes | Verify live contribution & tariff methodologies |
| **Art. 19 + 30(14)** | Audit clock tied to CNED notification | Enterprises claim “not notified”; CNED back-dates risk | Document notification; diary 1-year / 4-year cycle |
| **Art. 7¹(6)** | “At least a part” of measures for vulnerable households — share undefined | Programme challenge / donor conditionality fights | Lock a percentage in programme decisions |
| **Art. 27 priority dispatch** | Tension with L164 market-based redispatch | Curtailment allocation fights for CHP / RES | Contract for curtailment compensation; check market rules |
| **Law 282/2023 not in vault** | Building-performance duties only by cross-reference | Incomplete building advice from L139 alone | Pull 282/2023 before renovation / EPC building work |

## 5. Relevant practice & institutional map

* **[[CNED]]** — implementation, FEE, auditor register, SIMVE/SIME, savings M&V, ESCO list, EPC models.
* **Ministry of Energy** — policy, NECP draft, scheme design, EnC reporting.
* **[[ANRE]]** — obligated-party data, tariffs, investment plans, DR/dynamic pricing tools, CHP guarantees of origin interface.
* **National Inspectorate for Technical Supervision** — art. 28 sanctions.
* **[[Consiliul Concurenței]]** — art. 22(3) / 28(7) foreclosure of energy-services market.
* Public renovation programmes and local plans are **finance gates**, not just planning documents.

## 6. Client action checklist / compliance roadmap

- [ ] **Large enterprise:** confirm whether CNED has notified you; if yes, diary first audit (1 year) and 4-year cycle; check ISO 50001 / EMS exemption path (art. 19(2)).
- [ ] **DSO / petroleum importer:** confirm obligated-party status on ANRE’s quarterly list; model quarterly contribution cashflow if scheme active.
- [ ] **ESCO / EPC bidder:** use art. 23(5) checklist; for public clients confirm next-year budget line (art. 23(6)); for regulated counterparties map ANRE tariff treatment.
- [ ] **Public / municipal client:** check Annex 3 coverage, 3% renovation trajectory, local integrated plan as funding condition.
- [ ] **Developer / heat supplier:** multi-apartment metering / cost-allocator duties (art. 16²); 20 MW CBA trigger for thermal projects (art. 24).
- [ ] **Any electricity flexibility play:** do **not** stop at L139 — open L164/2025 licensing & aggregation chapters.
- [ ] **State-aid / double-funding:** if stacking FEE, Vulnerability Fund, and L10/2016 support, run Annex 2 no-double-counting check. → [[Concept — Ajutor de stat]]

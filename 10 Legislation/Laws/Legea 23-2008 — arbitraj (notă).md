# Legea nr. 23/2008 cu privire la arbitraj — Analysis
**Raw text:** [[Legea 23-2008 — arbitraj (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Hub:** [[MOC — Proceduri]] · **CPC:** [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (notă)]] · **Enforcement:** [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (notă)]] · **Civil code:** [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]]
> [!note] Core idea
> This domestic arbitration statute lets parties send arbitrable civil disputes outside ordinary courts, while courts remain essential for interim measures, set-aside, writs and enforcement.

## 1. Scope and definitions
Art. 1 applies to disputes from civil-law relationships, between parties with full capacity, where the parties agreed to arbitrate and the matter is arbitrable.
This is not judicial review of public-authority acts. Use [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]] unless a special law creates an arbitrable private-law dispute.
Art. 2 defines arbitration, arbitrator, competent court, arbitration agreement, dispute, arbitration clause and compromise.
The arbitration clause is separable: invalidity of the main contract does not automatically invalidate the clause.
The competent court is the ordinary court that would have heard the case under [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (notă)]] without arbitration.

## 2. Arbitrability
Art. 3 allows arbitration of patrimonial rights and non-patrimonial rights only where the parties may settle the matter.
Excluded areas include family-law claims, residential lease claims and patrimonial rights concerning housing.
Arbitrability matters at jurisdiction, set-aside and enforcement stages.

## 3. Principles and autonomy
Art. 4 lists legality, freedom of arbitral agreements, constitution according to party agreement, adversarial process, defence rights and confidentiality.
Art. 5 lets parties agree on appointment, replacement, procedure, place, term, costs and award form, subject to public order, good morals and mandatory law.
Art. 6 allows permanent institutions beside chambers, exchanges, unions, associations and other organisations, with notification to [[Curtea Supremă de Justiție]].
Ad hoc arbitration is allowed; arbitral institutions cannot be created beside central or local public authorities.

## 4. Arbitration agreement and court objection
Art. 8 requires written form under sanction of nullity. The agreement must indicate appointment method, any appointing authority, place and accepted rules.
Art. 9 is the court gateway: if court proceedings are filed despite an arbitration agreement, the objection must be raised no later than the first statement on the merits.
If timely raised, the court removes the case and sends it to arbitration unless the agreement is null, invalid or incapable of performance.

## 5. Court support
Art. 10 allows the competent court to order interim measures at any arbitration stage, including before initiation, under [[Codul de procedura civila 225-2003 (notă)]].
Art. 23 also allows court measures to preserve the subject matter or establish facts. These measures later interact with [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (notă)]].
Arbitration is private, but coercive protection still runs through the court/enforcement system.

## 6. Tribunal
Arts. 11–16 regulate arbitrators, appointment, acceptance, recusal, liability and termination.
Default tribunal: three arbitrators unless the parties chose otherwise.
Clauses giving one party unilateral appointment advantage are null. Arbitrators must disclose circumstances affecting impartiality or independence.
Appointment defects can later support set-aside or refusal of a writ.

## 7. Commencement and defence
Art. 17 requires a written claim with party data, reference to the arbitration agreement, facts, law, evidence, object, value, proposed arbitrator and signature.
Art. 18 starts arbitration on receipt of the claim and sets a default 6-month award term from tribunal constitution.
Art. 19 gives the respondent up to 30 days, or the agreed/institutional term, for defence. Exceptions not raised in time are generally lost.
Counterclaims must arise from the same legal relationship and be filed within the defence/first-appearance window.

## 8. Procedure, evidence and competence
Arts. 20–27 require impartial, practical and expeditious conduct, equal treatment, defence rights and adversarial procedure.
The tribunal manages citations, preparation, hearings, language, minutes, evidence and written-only proceedings where agreed.
Art. 27 gives competence-competence: the tribunal may decide jurisdiction and validity of the arbitration agreement, generally reviewable with the final award.

## 9. Award
Arts. 28–30 govern awards. An award may decide the merits, record termination or confirm settlement.
It must be written, signed, reasoned and sent within 10 days. Once communicated, it has effects of a final court judgment.
Correction, interpretation and additional awards are available. Coercive enforcement still needs a court writ.

## 10. Set-aside and enforcement
Art. 31 allows annulment within 3 months from receipt. The route is CPC arts. 477–481.
Grounds include non-arbitrability, null agreement, missing reasons/signatures/date, non-executable dispositive, improper tribunal/procedure, lack of notice, excess mandate and breach of fundamental principles or good morals.
Art. 33 makes the award binding and voluntarily executable. If not performed, the winner seeks a writ under CPC arts. 482–486 and enforces under [[Codul de executare 443-2004 (notă)]].
Foreign arbitral awards are handled under CPC arts. 475–476 and the New York Convention framework.

## Practitioner cautions
- Draft the clause in writing with rules, place and appointment mechanics.
- Check arbitrability before relying on the clause.
- Raise the arbitration objection early in court.
- Preserve proof of notice and appointment.
- Plan the CPC writ and enforcement step before the award.

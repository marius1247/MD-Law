# Legea nr. 74/2020 privind achizițiile în sectoarele energeticii, apei, transporturilor și serviciilor poștale — Analysis

**Raw text:** [[Legea 74-2020 — achizitii sectoriale (text)]] ✅ *complete* · **Energy hub:** [[MOC — Energetică]] · **Procedure:** [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]] · **Civil contracts:** [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]]

> [!note] Utilities procurement, not ordinary procurement
> This law implements the utilities procurement model: entities operating in energy, water, transport and postal sectors are subject to procurement rules because they control network/service activities of public interest, even when they are not classic public authorities.

## 1. What it is

L74/2020 is the special procurement law for utility sectors:

- energy;
- water;
- transport;
- postal services.

It transposes Directive 2014/25/EU as amended, including the 2026 harmonisation clause referencing Regulation (EU) 2023/2496. The text records LP102/2026 changes entering into force on 01.01.2027.

For this vault, the energy link is the most important: network operators and other energy-sector entities may be procurement-regulated even when their activity is not ordinary budget spending.

## 2. Scope and thresholds

Art. 1 regulates award of sectoral procurement contracts and design contests in the listed sectors, plus legal regime and certain performance aspects.

The current text records thresholds:

- goods/services and design contests — 800,000 MDL;
- works — 2,000,000 MDL;
- social and other specific services in annex no. 1 — 1,000,000 MDL.

It also records inserted higher-threshold particularities:

- goods/services — 8,000,000 MDL;
- works — 100,000,000 MDL;
- social/specific services — 19,000,000 MDL.

Because LP102/2026 enters into force on 01.01.2027, threshold timing must be checked for any live file.

## 3. Contracting authorities and entities

Art. 3 defines contracting authorities. Art. 4 defines contracting entities.

This distinction is central in utilities procurement:

- a classic public authority may be caught because of who it is;
- a utility undertaking may be caught because of what activity it performs and what special/exclusive rights it holds.

Do not assume a company is outside procurement law because it is organised as a company. In utilities sectors, the activity and rights matter.

## 4. Energy activities

Chapter II defines covered activities.

Art. 8 gives common provisions for sectoral activities.

Art. 9 covers **natural gas and heat**:

- provision or operation of fixed networks intended to provide public service in connection with production, transport or distribution of gas/heat;
- supply of gas or heat to such networks, subject to statutory conditions.

Art. 10 covers **electricity**:

- provision or operation of fixed networks intended to provide public service in connection with production, transport or distribution of electricity;
- supply of electricity to such networks, subject to statutory conditions.

This is the bridge to [[MOC — Energetică]]. Electricity, gas and heat procurement can fall here, not under ordinary public procurement.

## 5. Exceptions

Chapter III is long because utilities procurement has many carve-outs.

Important categories include:

- contracts awarded for purposes other than covered sectoral activity;
- resale/lease exceptions in some conditions;
- affiliated undertakings / joint venture exceptions;
- defence/security aspects;
- contracts subject to other rules;
- research and development;
- direct exposure to competition procedures.

Do not assume coverage merely because an energy company is buying something. Ask whether the purchase is for a covered activity and whether an exception applies.

## 6. Principles

Art. 28 states the procurement principles:

- efficient use of financial resources;
- transparency;
- equal treatment;
- non-discrimination;
- proportionality;
- mutual recognition;
- assumption of responsibility.

These principles are litigation tools. A tender condition can be unlawful even if no article expressly forbids that exact wording, if it violates proportionality, equal treatment or competition.

## 7. Procedures

Art. 36 lists procedures:

- open tender;
- restricted tender;
- competitive negotiation;
- negotiated procedure without prior publication;
- competitive dialogue;
- innovation partnership;
- other special procedures/methods allowed by the law.

Then:

- art. 37 — open tender;
- art. 38 — restricted tender;
- art. 39 — competitive negotiation;
- art. 40 — negotiated procedure without prior publication;
- art. 41 — competitive dialogue;
- art. 42 — innovation partnership.

Utilities procurement is more flexible than ordinary procurement, but flexibility is structured. The entity must still fit the chosen procedure into statutory conditions.

## 8. Frameworks, dynamic systems, e-tools

Chapter VI covers special award methods:

- art. 43 — framework agreements;
- art. 44 — dynamic purchasing systems;
- art. 45 — electronic auction;
- art. 46 — electronic catalogue;
- arts. 47–48¹ — centralised, occasional joint and cross-border/joint procurement mechanisms;
- art. 49 — procurement file.

Art. 49 matters in disputes: the file should reveal the entity's reasoning, communications, evaluation and compliance path.

## 9. Market consultation and specifications

Chapter VII contains the tender-design rules:

- art. 50 — market consultation;
- art. 51 — prior involvement of bidders;
- art. 52 — rules on description of goods, works and services;
- art. 53 — labels;
- art. 54 — electronic availability of procurement documents;
- art. 55 — offers and alternatives;
- art. 56 — lots;
- art. 57 — deadlines.

For energy infrastructure procurement, art. 52 is often central. Technical specifications must describe the need without unjustifiably favouring a supplier or technology.

## 10. Transparency

Chapter VIII covers:

- art. 59 — prior information notice;
- art. 60 — contract notice;
- art. 61 — award notice;
- art. 62 — qualification systems;
- arts. 62¹–62² — additional notice/publication mechanisms inserted later;
- art. 64 — information and award communication;
- art. 65 — contract-conclusion deadlines.

Transparency is especially important in energy because procurement often involves large network investments, losses procurement, emergency supply and technically complex assets.

## 11. Selection and award

Chapter IX governs selection:

- qualification systems — art. 67;
- qualification and selection criteria — art. 68;
- reliance on third-party capacity — art. 69;
- use of criteria — art. 70;
- quality standards — art. 71;
- self-declarations — art. 72.

Chapter X governs award:

- art. 76 — award criteria;
- art. 77 — life-cycle costing;
- art. 78 — abnormally low tenders.

Life-cycle costing is particularly relevant for energy assets. A cheap transformer, meter, IT system or grid component may be more expensive over its operational life.

## 12. Contract performance and modification

Chapter XI addresses the contract after award:

- art. 79 — performance conditions;
- art. 80 — sectoral procurement contract;
- art. 81 — subcontracting;
- art. 82 — modification of the procurement contract;
- art. 83 — termination;
- art. 84 — procurement report.

Art. 82 is one of the highest-risk provisions. Contract modification after award can become an unlawful direct award if it changes the economic balance or scope beyond statutory limits.

For private-law consequences, also check [[Codul civil 1107-2002 (notă)]]. Procurement law governs whether the contract could be awarded/modified; civil law governs many performance consequences.

## 13. Remedies and institutions

Chapter XIII covers competence and responsibilities. Art. 87 identifies the National Agency for Solving Complaints / procurement-remedy authority. Art. 87¹ adds further institutional rules.

Remedy decisions may themselves become administrative-law litigation questions. Read with [[Codul administrativ 116-2018 (notă)]], especially admissibility, suspension and judicial-review limits.

## 14. Energy-specific use cases

Use this law for:

- procurement by electricity/gas/heat network operators;
- purchases connected to transmission/distribution infrastructure;
- procurement of electricity/gas/heat-related works and technical services;
- procurement for network losses where sectoral rules require market-based procedures;
- large metering, SCADA, dispatch, balancing or grid-development procurements;
- procurement by energy entities operating under special/exclusive rights.

Read with:

- [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)]];
- [[HANRE 283-2020 — Regulile pietei energiei electrice (notă)]];
- [[HANRE 311-2026 — racordarea la retelele electrice (notă)]];
- [[HANRE 420-2019 — Codul retelelor de gaze naturale (notă)]];
- [[Concept — Unbundling]] where affiliated undertakings and network operators are involved.

## 15. Relationship to regulated tariffs

Procurement costs may feed regulated tariffs if they are accepted as justified/efficient regulated costs.

That creates a double control:

1. procurement legality under L74/2020;
2. tariff recoverability under the relevant tariff methodology, e.g. [[HANRE 626-2023 — Metodologie tarife transport EE (notă)]].

A cost can be incurred contractually but still disputed in tariff regulation if inefficient, unjustified or outside approved investment plans.

## 16. Relationship to emergencies

Energy markets sometimes require urgent procurement, especially during supply crises. Art. 40 negotiated procedure without prior publication may be tempting, but urgency is not a magic word. The factual and legal conditions must be documented.

Read with crisis/supply-security rules and any Government emergency acts before using emergency procurement paths.

## 17. Practitioner cautions

- Identify whether the purchaser is a contracting authority, contracting entity or outside scope.
- Identify whether the procurement is for a covered sectoral activity.
- Check exceptions before assuming the law applies.
- Preserve the procurement file; it is the litigation record.
- Treat technical specifications as competition-sensitive.
- Be careful with contract modification under art. 82.
- Check LP102/2026 effective dates for thresholds and harmonisation wording.

## 18. Relationship to repealed ANRE procurement regulation

ANRE’s former licence-holder procurement regulation [[HANRE 24-2017 — achizitii titulari de licenta (abrogata) (notă)|HANRE 24/2017]] was **abrogated** by [[HANRE 305-2021 — abrogare HANRE 24-2017 achizitii titulari (notă)|HANRE 305/2021]] expressly on **art. 89(3)** of this law (IF 06.08.2021). Live utilities procurement advice starts here — not with HANRE 24.

# Accounting & financial reporting — synthesis

The reasoning layer for accounting law. Tax side: [[Fiscalitate — synthesis]]. Hub: [[MOC — Fiscalitate & Contabilitate]]. Horizon / reforms / gaps: [[Fiscalitate & Contabilitate — horizon and gaps]].

---

## 1. Why accounting law is legal, not technical

Financial statements are not an internal management artefact. They are the input to at least five legal determinations:

| Determination | Depends on the accounts |
|---|---|
| **Taxable profit** | Starts from accounting profit and is adjusted → [[Fiscalitate — synthesis]] |
| **Distributable profit** | What may lawfully be paid out to owners → [[Concept — Adunarea generală]] |
| **The net-assets trigger** | Net assets below share capital forces a decision → [[Concept — Capital social]] |
| **Insolvency in fact** | The forensic question of *when* → [[Concept — Insolvabilitate]] |
| **Director liability** | Failure to keep proper accounts is itself a breach → [[Concept — Administrator]] |

So the accounting regime is the plumbing under company law and tax law both. Get it wrong and everything downstream is wrong.

## 2. The framework

**Legea contabilității și raportării financiare nr. 287/2017** — replaced the previous accounting law and rebuilt the regime on the EU **Accounting Directive 2013/34/EU** model. Its central move is **proportionality**: obligations scale with entity size. → [[Legea 287-2017 — contabilitate (notă)]] ✅ ingested

Two reporting frameworks sit beneath it:
- **SNC** — *Standardele Naționale de Contabilitate*, the national standards — gateway approving order [[OMF 118-2013 — Standardele Nationale de Contabilitate (notă)|OMF 118/2013]] ✅; **individual SNC texts still not ingested**
- **IFRS** — mandatory for **public interest entities**; available to others by election

**Public interest entities** — banks, insurers, and other entities designated by law — always report under **IFRS**, regardless of size. Size only determines the regime for everyone else.

Amending act: **[[Legea 86-2026 — modificare Legea contabilitatii (notă)|Legea nr. 86/2026]]** ✅ ingested — threshold reset **IF 1 January 2027**.

## 3. The size categories — and the 2027 reset

Categorisation works on the EU pattern: an entity falls in a category if it does not exceed **two of three** criteria (total assets, sales revenue, average employees) on the balance sheet date, with a consistency rule so that a single unusual year does not reclassify an entity.

> [!important] New thresholds from **1 January 2027** — model **now**
> **Legea nr. 86 of 21 May 2026** amends L287/2017, raising the criteria substantially — an inflation and EU-alignment adjustment tracking the 2023 revision of Directive 2013/34/EU. Transition uses the position at **31 December 2026** for calendar-year entities.
>
> | Category | Total assets | Sales revenue |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Micro** | ≤ MDL 8,500,000 | ≤ MDL 17,000,000 |
> | **Small** | ≤ MDL 95,000,000 | ≤ MDL 190,000,000 |
> | **Medium** | ≤ MDL 480,000,000 | ≤ MDL 960,000,000 |
> | **Large** | above medium | above medium |
>
> Verify the **employee-headcount criterion** and the exact transitional rules against the enacted text before applying. *(Verified July / Aug 2026.)*

**Why this matters commercially.** A large number of Moldovan companies will move **down** a category — from small to micro, from medium to small. That reduces reporting obligations, audit exposure and consolidation duties in one step. For any client near a boundary, **model the 2027 change in 2026**: it may remove an audit requirement entirely.

**Keep separate from the draft fiscal rewrite.** L86 is enacted accounting law. The 2027 Estonian-style CIT draft is a different instrument and **not law** — see [[Fiscalitate & Contabilitate — horizon and gaps]]. Accounting thresholds do not change CIT rates.

**Groups** are categorised on the same pattern. **Small and medium groups are exempt from consolidated financial statements and consolidated management reports** — unless a member is a public interest entity, which pulls the whole group back into consolidation.

## 4. The obligations, by tier
Roughly, in ascending order of size:

- **Bookkeeping** — double-entry as the norm; simplified options for the smallest entities
- **Financial statements** — abridged for micro and small entities; **complete statements under SNC** for medium and large; **IFRS** for public interest entities
- **Management report** — from small/medium upward
- **Statutory audit** — driven by category and by sector, and independently required for public interest entities
- **Filing and publication** — annual submission; the state financial-reporting system is the collection point
- **Consolidation** — large groups, and any group containing a public interest entity

→ [[Concept — Situații financiare]]

## 5. Practical points that cause real problems
- **Accounting profit ≠ taxable profit.** The Fiscal Code adjusts: non-deductible expenses, differing depreciation, provisions, related-party pricing, art. 87(11) deferral. Always keep the reconciliation — it is the first thing an [[SFS]] inspector asks for. Bridge work still thin — see horizon gaps.
- **The chief accountant / responsible person** has statutory duties, but **responsibility for the accounts rests with management**. Delegation to an external accounting firm does not transfer it.
- **Document retention and primary documents.** Most fiscal control findings are evidential — the transaction was real but the supporting document was missing or defective.
- **Currency.** Accounts in MDL; foreign-currency transactions translated with exchange differences recognised. In a volatile-currency environment this is a material P&L item, not a footnote.

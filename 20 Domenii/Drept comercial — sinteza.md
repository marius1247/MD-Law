# Commercial law — synthesis

The reasoning layer for commercial law. Act inventory: [[MOC — Drept comercial]].

---

## 1. The boundary — and why it needs stating

Moldova has **no commercial code**. It follows the **monist** model: the Civil Code governs both civil and commercial relations, with special rules layered on for business actors. There is therefore no clean statutory line to point at, and the domain has to be defined by its subject matter.

The working division used in this vault:

| Hub | Governs |
|---|---|
| [[MOC — Societăți & Guvernanță corporativă]] | **The entity** — formation, organs, capital, ownership, failure |
| [[MOC — Drept civil]] | **The general law of obligations** — contract formation, performance, remedies, property |
| **This hub** | **The activity** — the right to trade, market conduct, competition, sector authorisation, insolvency as market exit |

Overlap is unavoidable. When in doubt: if the question is *who owns and controls*, it is company law; if it is *may this business do this, and on what terms*, it is here.

## 2. The right to carry on business

**Constitutional footing.** Economic freedom and free enterprise are constitutionally protected, which makes disproportionate restriction on business activity a reviewable question rather than purely a policy one. → [[Constituția RM — text]]

**The statutory layer** is in transition:

- **Legea nr. 845/1992** cu privire la antreprenoriat și întreprinderi — the Soviet-era framework act. Still formally in force, riddled with provisions that contradict the Civil Code, and **slated for complete repeal**. A Government project (345/MDED/2025) replaces it with a new entrepreneurship law, entering into force **two years after publication** to allow related legislation to be adapted. Treat 845/1992 as legacy: before relying on any provision, check whether the Civil Code has already displaced it.
- **Legea nr. 235/2006** privind principiile de bază de reglementare a activității de întreprinzător — the **better-regulation statute**. It sets the principles that constrain the state when it regulates business: proportionality, predictability, transparency, regulatory impact assessment, the "silence is consent" default, and the prohibition on requirements not grounded in law. Underused, and genuinely useful as an argument against an unlawful administrative requirement.
- **Legea nr. 160/2011** privind reglementarea prin autorizare a activității de întreprinzător — the **permits framework**: the exhaustive list of authorisations, the one-stop-shop principle, time limits, and the rule that no permit may be demanded unless it is on the list.

→ [[Concept — Autorizarea activității de întreprinzător]]

## 3. Contracts in a commercial setting

Governed by the **Codul civil nr. 1107/2002**, Cartea III (obligations), substantially modernised by the 2018–19 civil law reform which drew on the DCFR and on German-model doctrine.

The commercially significant features:
- **Freedom of contract** as the default, with professional-party rules that are stricter than consumer rules in some respects and looser in others
- **Standard terms** control — incorporation and unfairness review
- **Good faith** in negotiation and performance, including pre-contractual liability
- **Remedies** — specific performance as a genuine primary remedy, alongside termination, price reduction and damages
- **Security** — pledge over movables and receivables, registered; guarantees; retention of title
- **Prescription** — limitation periods that differ from the general civil ones in specific commercial contexts

**Practical point.** The 2018–19 reform is recent enough that a good deal of pre-reform commentary and habit remains in circulation. Verify that any authority relied on post-dates the reform.

→ [[MOC — Drept civil]]

## 4. Competition — the market-conduct layer

**Legea concurenței nr. 183/2012**, enforced by the **[[Consiliul Concurenței]]**, transposes the EU competition model almost directly. Four pillars:

1. **Anticompetitive agreements** — the analogue of Art. 101 TFEU. Horizontal cartels (price fixing, market sharing, bid rigging) are the hard core; vertical restraints are assessed more permissively.
2. **Abuse of dominance** — the analogue of Art. 102. Dominance is not unlawful; leveraging it is. → [[Concept — Poziție dominantă]]
3. **Merger control** — mandatory notification above turnover thresholds, with **standstill**: closing before clearance is itself an infringement, independent of any substantive harm.
4. **State aid** — Moldova is unusual outside the EU in having a full state aid regime, a direct consequence of the Association Agreement. → [[Concept — Ajutor de stat]]

Separately, **unfair competition** (misleading practices, confusion, denigration, misuse of trade secrets) sits alongside the antitrust rules.

**Where this bites hardest in practice: bid rigging in public procurement**, which is simultaneously a competition infringement and a procurement problem. → [[MOC — Achiziții publice & Statul]]

## 5. Insolvency as market exit
**Legea insolvabilității nr. 149/2012** is treated in this vault as part of the company-law domain, because that is where the liability sits. Commercially it is the **exit mechanism**: the rule that replaces the creditors' race with a collective process, and the reason unsecured commercial credit is priced the way it is. → [[Concept — Insolvabilitate]]

## 6. Adjacent regimes worth knowing exist
- **Consumer protection** — where the counterparty is a consumer, mandatory rules displace freedom of contract
- **Intellectual property** — trademarks, designs, patents; **AGEPI** as the office
- **E-commerce and electronic signature** — increasingly the default channel, with its own formality rules
- **Foreign trade and customs** — the DCFTA regime under the Association Agreement, plus the **Codul vamal** → [[Fiscalitate — sinteza sistemului fiscal]]
- **Sector-specific regulation** — banking (BNM), non-bank finance ([[CNPF]]), **energy ([[ANRE]])**. Sector rules displace the general commercial rules within their perimeter → [[Energetică — sinteza sectorului]]

## 7. The through-line: EU alignment
Almost everything in this domain moves under the **Association Agreement and DCFTA**, and now under accession preparation. Competition, state aid, consumer protection, technical regulation and standards are all being pulled toward the acquis. Constitutionally this is reinforced by [[Constituția RM — text|art. 140¹]], which gives binding EU acts priority over contrary domestic law on accession.

**The practical corollary:** where a Moldovan provision is transposed from an EU instrument — and most of the competition rules are — **EU case law is the best available interpretive guide**, and is treated as persuasive.

# Concept — Administrator

**RO:** administrator / organ executiv · **EN:** director / executive body

## Definition
The **executive organ**: runs the company's day-to-day business and **represents it toward third parties**. May be a single person or a collegial body. The default and residual competence sits here — whatever is not reserved to the general meeting or the board falls to the administrator.

## Two distinct capacities — keep them apart
1. **Representation (external).** The administrator's power to bind the company toward third parties is **registered with [[ASP]]** and, as a rule, cannot be cut down against a good-faith third party by internal restrictions that are not registered. A counterparty verifies authority in the **register**, not in a board minute.
2. **Management (internal).** Internally the administrator is bound by the constitutive act, board decisions and the mandate. Exceeding those limits is a breach generating **liability**, even where the transaction itself binds the company.

The gap between the two is deliberate: it protects transactional certainty for third parties while preserving accountability internally. Most administrator-liability cases live in that gap.

## Duties
- **Loyalty** — act in the company's interest, not one's own or a shareholder's; **disclose conflicts** and stand aside
- **Diligence** — informed, careful decision-making; a defensible process rather than a guaranteed outcome
- **Compliance** — accounting and financial reporting, tax filings, registration updates, sector obligations → [[Concept — Situații financiare]] · [[Fiscalitate — sinteza sistemului fiscal]]
- **Act on the net-assets trigger** — where net assets fall below share capital → [[Concept — Capital social]]
- **File in time** — the duty to petition for insolvency once the company is insolvent → [[Concept — Insolvabilitate]]

## Exposure — where administrators actually get hurt
Ranked by how often it bites in practice:

1. **Late insolvency filing.** Continuing to trade and incur liabilities after the company is insolvent in fact. The gap between actual insolvency and the filing date is the liability.
2. **Transactions in the suspect period** — preferences and undervalue transfers, unwound and attributed personally.
3. **Tax and contribution defaults** — where the fiscal regime reaches the responsible person.
4. **Related-party transactions** entered without the required approval.
5. **Acting without registered authority** — an expired or unregistered mandate.

## Appointment and termination
Appointed by the general meeting or the board depending on form and constitutive act; the mandate and its termination are **registered**. Registration is the practical moment of change for third parties — an unregistered removal leaves the former administrator apparently able to bind the company.

## Notes / conclusions
> In due diligence, always pull the **current registered administrator** from ASP rather than relying on the file. And when advising an administrator of a struggling company, the first question is not commercial — it is whether the filing duty has already been triggered.

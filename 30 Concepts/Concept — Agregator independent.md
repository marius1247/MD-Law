# Concept — Agregator independent

**RO:** agregator independent · **EN:** independent aggregator

## Definition
A market participant holding an **aggregation licence** that aggregates under L164 and is **not affiliated to its customer’s supplier**. The “independent” qualifier exists to stop incumbent foreclosure of demand-response / flexibility.
> Defined in [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)#Articolul 2. Noțiuni|L164 art. 2 pt. 6]]. Aggregation generally: art. 2 (agregare) · operative [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (text)#Articolul 120. Agregarea|arts. 120–121, 124]].

## How it works
**Licence:** aggregation is a regulated activity (art. 16(1)(i)) with its own licence type (art. 18(1)(i)), term **10 years** (art. 18(10)).

**Contract (art. 120):** aggregation rests on a contract between final customer and aggregator. Customers may contract **without the supplier’s consent**. For the contract’s duration, rights/obligations on demand-response trading, compensation, pricing and settlement on the relevant markets **transfer to the aggregator**. Minimum contract content is statutory (parties, object, settlement, liability, data protection, term, amendment, termination).

**Notice:** aggregator informs the relevant TSO/DSO and the customer’s supplier of the aggregation contract (art. 120(8)).

**Switching:** changing aggregator follows the same consumer-protection logic as supplier switching — ≤ **20 days**, no switching fee as a rule, capacity-follows-customer principle on network contracts when changing supplier (art. 121). ANRE must approve a dedicated switching regulation.

**Demand response via aggregation (art. 124):** aggregators (including independents) may participate on electricity markets **without consent of other market participants**; ANRE must put non-discrimination, data-exchange, anti-penalty and dispute-resolution rules into the market rules; aggregators are [[Concept — Parte responsabilă de echilibrare|PRE]] or delegate; activation may trigger limited financial compensation to affected suppliers/BRPs.

**Secondary gap:** art. 120(9) requires an ANRE aggregation regulation — track adoption; until then advise from the statute + market rules as far as they go.

## Notes / conclusions
> Any supplier-owned aggregator is still an aggregator — but it is **not** an *independent* aggregator. Independence is a competition-protection status, not a separate licence class.

# Concept — Ajutor de stat

**RO:** ajutor de stat · **EN:** state aid

## Definition
Any advantage granted by the state or through state resources to an undertaking on a **selective** basis, which distorts or threatens to distort competition and affects trade. Moldova operates a **full state aid regime** — unusual for a non-EU state — enforced by the **[[Consiliul Concurenței]]**, and it exists because the **Association Agreement** required it.

## The four cumulative conditions
All four must be met. Failing any one takes the measure outside the regime:

1. **State resources** — a transfer of public resources, or forgone revenue (a tax exemption counts)
2. **Advantage** — a benefit the undertaking would not obtain on normal market terms
3. **Selectivity** — favouring certain undertakings or the production of certain goods. A genuinely general measure applying to all is not aid
4. **Distortion of competition and effect on trade**

The **market economy operator test** is the practical workhorse: would a rational private investor, creditor or vendor, in the same position, have done the same on the same terms? If yes, there is no advantage and therefore no aid. This is how public capital injections, loans, guarantees and land sales are assessed.

## Forms it takes — often invisibly
Grants are the obvious case and the rarest problem. The ones that catch people out:

- **Tax exemptions, reduced rates and deferrals** → [[Fiscalitate — synthesis]]
- **Guarantees** given below a market fee
- **Capital injections** into state-owned enterprises on non-market terms
- **Sale or lease of public land or assets** below market value
- **Debt write-offs**, including of tax and social contribution arrears
- **Regulated tariffs and compensation for public service obligations** set above the net cost

## Procedure
- **Notification and standstill** — aid must be notified and, in principle, **not implemented before clearance**
- **Unlawful aid** may be ordered **recovered from the beneficiary, with interest**. This is the sharp end: the recipient, not the granting authority, bears the loss
- **Exemptions and de minimis** thresholds relieve small or block-exempted categories from full procedure
- **Reporting and transparency** obligations on granting authorities

## Where this matters most in this vault
**Energy.** The whole support architecture is potential state aid:

- **Renewables support** — fixed tariffs and guaranteed offtake for [[Concept — Producător eligibil|eligible producers]] are a textbook aid measure, justified where they satisfy the environmental and energy aid conditions
- **Public service obligation compensation** — compensating a supplier for a PSO is aid unless it satisfies the *Altmark*-style conditions: a clearly defined obligation, objective and transparent compensation parameters set in advance, no overcompensation, and either a competitive selection or an efficient-operator benchmark. → [[Concept — Furnizor de ultimă opțiune]]
- **Support to state-owned network operators** — capital and guarantees to entities like the TSO

→ [[Energetică — synthesis]] · [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]]

The same analysis applies to **IT Park** and other sectoral tax regimes, and to any negotiated investment incentive.

## Notes / conclusions
> The recovery risk sits with the **beneficiary**. Any client accepting a public advantage — a tax break, a below-market asset, a PSO compensation stream — should have the state aid analysis done before accepting it, not after a complaint is filed.

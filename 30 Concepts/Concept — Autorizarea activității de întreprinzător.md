# Concept — Autorizarea activității de întreprinzător

**RO:** autorizarea activității de întreprinzător · **EN:** business authorisation / permits regime

## Definition
The framework governing **when the state may require a permit** to carry on a business activity, and how such permits are granted. Principal act: **Legea nr. 160/2011 privind reglementarea prin autorizare a activității de întreprinzător**, sitting on the principles in **Legea nr. 235/2006**.

## The core rule — and why it matters
Permits may only be required if they appear on the **exhaustive statutory list (nomenclator)**. An authority may **not invent a requirement**, however reasonable it sounds. This single rule is the most useful thing in the regime, and it is regularly ignored at the counter.

Supporting guarantees:
- **One-stop shop** — the applicant deals with one point of contact, not with each contributing authority
- **Statutory time limits** for decision, with **tacit approval ("silence is consent")** where the authority fails to decide in time — subject to the exceptions the law sets out
- **Reasoned refusal** and a right of appeal
- **Fees limited to cost** — a permit fee is not a tax
- **Regulatory impact assessment** before a new requirement is introduced → Legea nr. 235/2006

## Three things people conflate
| | What it authorises | Granted by |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration** | **Existence** of the legal person | [[ASP]] — constitutive |
| **Permit / authorisation** | The **activity** | The competent authority, per the nomenclator |
| **Sector licence** | A **regulated activity** in a supervised sector | The sector regulator, e.g. [[ANRE]] |

A registered company is not thereby permitted to do everything; a permit is not a licence; and a licence does not remove the need for other permits. → [[Concept — Licență în energetică]]

## The regulatory-principles layer (Legea nr. 235/2006)
Underused and worth deploying. It binds the state when it regulates business:

- **proportionality** — the restriction must be no more than the objective requires
- **predictability and transparency** — no surprise requirements, adequate notice
- **legality** — no obligation without a basis in law
- **regulatory impact assessment** for new burdens
- **presumption in favour of the good-faith operator**

These are not aspirations. They are grounds of challenge to an administrative act, argued through the **Codul administrativ nr. 116/2018**. → [[MOC — Proceduri]]

## Practical sequence for a new business
1. **Register** with [[ASP]] — the entity comes into existence → [[Societăți & guvernanță — synthesis]]
2. **Tax and VAT registration** with [[SFS]] → [[Concept — TVA]]
3. **Identify permits** from the nomenclator for the specific activity — and refuse anything not on it
4. **Sector licence**, if the activity is regulated
5. Ancillary: premises, sanitary, environmental, fire safety, as applicable

## The transition to watch
**Legea nr. 845/1992** — the old entrepreneurship framework — is slated for **complete repeal** by a new entrepreneurship law (Government project 345/MDED/2025), taking effect **two years after publication**. It will reshape the general layer above the permits regime. Track it before relying on 845/1992 for anything.

## Notes / conclusions
> When a client is told they need a permit, the first question is not how to obtain it — it is **whether it is on the nomenclator at all**. A surprising proportion of demanded requirements are not.

# Concept — Consiliul societății

**RO:** consiliul societății / consiliul de administrație · **EN:** board

## Definition
The organ standing **between the general meeting and the executive**: it supervises management, decides matters too significant for the administrator but not reserved to the owners, and prepares the meeting's business.

- **SA** — a board is **mandatory**, together with an executive body and a censor/audit function. Three organs, deliberately.
- **SRL** — a board is **optional**. Most SRLs run on general meeting + administrator alone.

## Why a board exists
The general meeting is too infrequent and too diffuse to supervise a manager; the manager cannot supervise himself. The board is the standing organ that closes the gap — continuous oversight without operational involvement.

## Typical competence
- appointing, remunerating and removing the executive
- approving budgets, strategy and significant transactions below the general-meeting threshold
- convening the general meeting and preparing its agenda
- controlling **related-party transactions** — the board's most substantive gatekeeping function
- oversight of accounting, reporting and the audit relationship → [[Concept — Situații financiare]]

## Duties and liability
Members owe duties of **loyalty** and **diligence**, and are liable for damage caused by breach. In practice the enforceable core is procedural:

- **Disclose conflicts** and abstain from the affected decision
- **Decide on an informed basis** — the record of what was before the board is the defence
- **Act within competence** — decisions outside it are vulnerable
- **Minute properly.** A dissent that is not minuted did not happen. Board minutes are the single most important liability document in the company.

## Governance quality — the honest read
Moldovan boards in closely-held companies are frequently formal. The controlling owner is also the decision-maker, and the board ratifies. The provisions that actually bite are therefore not the aspirational governance norms but the **hard ones**: related-party transaction control, the net-assets duty, and duties in the vicinity of insolvency. Focus there.

The **EU alignment** trajectory — Directive (EU) 2017/1132 and Directive 2007/36/EC — is pushing toward stronger disclosure and shareholder engagement, with remuneration policy for persons holding positions of responsibility a live legislative file.

## Notes / conclusions
> Ask two questions of any board: does it have the information to supervise, and does it have the independence to act on it. In most closely-held Moldovan companies the answer to the second is no — so plan enforcement around the shareholders and the creditors, not the board.

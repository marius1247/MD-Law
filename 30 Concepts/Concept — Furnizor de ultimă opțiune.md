# Concept — Furnizor de ultimă opțiune

**RO:** furnizor de ultimă opțiune · **EN:** supplier of last resort

> [!danger] Base act replaced — 19 August 2025
> References below to **Legea 107/2016** are to a **repealed** statute. It was abrogated in full by [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|Legea nr. 164/2025]], which transposes the EU **fourth package** (Dir. 2019/944, Reg. 2019/943), **REMIT** and **CACM**. Article numbering is entirely different, and new regulated activities — **storage, aggregation, trading** — were added. Verify every L107 citation against L164/2025 before use.

## Definition
A supplier **designated to take over consumers who would otherwise be left without supply** — because their supplier lost its licence, went insolvent, exited, or because the consumer never actively chose one. The mechanism that makes it safe to liberalise a market in an essential service.

## The problem it solves
Liberalisation means suppliers can fail. In a market for shoes, failure means the customer shops elsewhere at leisure. In a market for electricity, failure means an unheated house. So the law inserts a **default counterparty**: supply never stops, it is simply transferred, and the commercial and legal consequences are sorted out afterwards.

## How it works
- **Designation** — a supplier is designated for a defined area or category of consumers, by or under [[ANRE]]'s procedure. Designation is an obligation, not an opportunity, and it is therefore a **public service obligation** in the sense of [[Legea 107-2016 — energia electrica (text)#Articolul 11. Obligaţii de serviciu public|L107 art. 11]].
- **Automatic transfer** — on the trigger event, affected consumers pass to the designated supplier **without needing to contract**; a contract is deemed to arise. This is the whole point: no gap, no consumer action required.
- **Price** — supplied at a regulated or otherwise constrained tariff, typically set so as not to advantage last-resort supply over the competitive market. → [[Concept — Tarif reglementat]]
- **Temporary character** — the consumer is expected to move to a chosen supplier; the last-resort relationship is a bridge, often time-limited.
- **Compensation** — where the obligation imposes net cost, the PSO framework provides for recovery.

## Distinguish from *universal service*
- **Universal service** = a standing right of household and small consumers to be supplied at a specified quality and price.
- **Supplier of last resort** = the *contingency* mechanism that operates when normal supply fails.

They overlap in practice — often the same undertaking discharges both — but they are different obligations with different triggers, and conflating them is a common drafting error.

## Where this is heading
Public service obligations are being **narrowed**. The withdrawal of PSOs for large industrial gas consumers, run from October 2025 toward completion on **1 April 2026**, is the current instance. The pattern to expect: large consumers fully exposed to the market; last-resort and universal-service protection concentrated on households and small consumers.

## Notes / conclusions
> When a supplier fails, the legally interesting questions are not about the consumers — those are handled automatically. They are about **who bears the cost of the transferred portfolio**, and whether the last-resort supplier can recover it. Read the compensation mechanism first.

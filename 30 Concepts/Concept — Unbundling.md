# Concept — Unbundling

**RO:** separarea activităţilor / decuplare · **EN:** unbundling

> Electricity unbundling / ownership separation now in [[Legea 164-2025 — energia electrica (notă)|L164/2025]] (e.g. arts. **26**, **29**, **61**, accounting separation art. **133**). Gas remains under [[Legea 108-2016 — gazele naturale (notă)|L108]] third-package architecture. L107 cites are historical only.

## Definition
The mandatory **separation of network activities** (transmission, distribution) **from competitive activities** (generation, supply, trading) inside a vertically integrated undertaking. Originally imported via third-package L107/L108; electricity now restated under the fourth-package L164.

## Why it exists
A firm that owns the wires and also sells energy has both the ability and the incentive to disadvantage rival suppliers — through access terms, connection queues, investment choices, and cross-subsidy of its own retail arm from the regulated network business. Behavioural remedies (non-discrimination duties, regulatory oversight) have a poor record against this, because the regulator can never see enough. The structural remedy removes the incentive instead of policing it.

## The ladder of models
From weakest to strongest, all present in the EU acquis and reflected in Moldovan law:

1. **Accounting unbundling** — separate accounts per activity. Minimum tier; applies broadly, including to distribution.
2. **Functional / management unbundling** — separate decision-making and staff, independence of network management from the parent's commercial arm.
3. **Legal unbundling** — the network operator is a **separate legal entity**. Ownership may still be common.
4. **Ownership unbundling (OU)** — the network owner may not hold control in generation or supply. The strongest, and the EU's preferred model.
   - Alternatives where OU is not adopted: **ISO** (Independent System Operator) and **ITO** (Independent Transmission Operator), each with a compensating package of independence safeguards and compliance machinery.

**Certification** is the enforcement gate: an operator cannot be designated as a TSO until the regulator certifies compliance with the chosen model, and third-country control triggers an additional security-of-supply test.

## Where it bites in Moldova
- **Electricity** — Î.S. **Moldelectrica** as TSO; distribution largely with the Premier Energy group. The certification and independence questions attach here.
- **Gas** — the harder case. The historic **Moldovagaz** structure with Gazprom ownership was the central obstacle; transport was carved out to **Vestmoldtransgaz**. Gas unbundling in Moldova is therefore not a technical compliance exercise but a **geopolitical** one, and it is the file where domestic law, the Energy Community acquis, and external ownership collide most directly.
- Anything written on Moldovan gas unbundling before 2023 is stale. Verify the current corporate perimeter before relying on it.

## Corporate-law mechanics
Unbundling is executed through ordinary company law: spin-offs, contributions in kind, transfers of going concerns, shareholder-agreement changes, and governance ring-fencing.

# Harta instituțională (state governance map)

Who holds power and issues which acts. Grounded in **Titlul III** of the [[Constituția RM (text)|Constitution]].

## Legislative
- [[Parlamentul RM]] — unicameral, 101 MPs; adopts laws & Parliament decisions

## Head of state
- [[Președintele RM]] — promulgates laws, issues decrees, foreign affairs & defence roles

## Executive
- [[Guvernul RM]] — decisions (hotărâri) & ordinances (ordonanțe)
- **Ministries** — sectoral normative acts (e.g. Ministerul Energiei, Ministerul Finanțelor)
- **Autonomous regulators** — [[ANRE]] (energy), BNM (central bank), [[CNPF]] (non-bank financial markets), [[Consiliul Concurenței]] (competition & state aid)
- **Agencies** — [[ASP]] (registries), [[SFS]] (tax), [[CNED]] (energy efficiency / FEE under Ministry of Energy), Agenția Achiziții Publice, ANSC

## Judicial
- Ordinary courts: judecătorii → curți de apel → **Curtea Supremă de Justiție**
- [[Curtea Constituțională]] — constitutional review (separate from the ordinary hierarchy)

## Territorial
- Autonomous territorial unit **Găgăuzia** (special status) · local public administration (raioane, municipii, sate/comune)

## Why the autonomous regulators sit apart
[[ANRE]], BNM, [[CNPF]] and [[Consiliul Concurenței]] are deliberately insulated from Government: fixed terms, exhaustive removal grounds, own funding, accountability to Parliament rather than to a minister. The reason is the same in each case — they take decisions (tariffs, monetary policy, fines against state bodies, state aid recovery) that a politically dependent body could not take. Independence is also an **EU acquis requirement**, not a domestic design preference. → [[Legea 174-2017 — energetica (notă)]] for the worked example.
